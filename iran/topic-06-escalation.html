Iran at War · Topic 6 · Return toward today · 2023–2026

From Shadow War to Open War

Iran and Israel had been bitter rivals for decades without fighting a sustained direct war. The key question is not why they disliked one another. It is why the old limits on direct attacks broke down.

Essential question

At what point did full-scale war become substantially more likely?

Do not just pick the largest attack. Identify the moment that changed what each side believed it could do next.

Step 2 · Find the old limit

For years, much of the conflict stayed below the threshold of open war.

Iran and Israel fought through covert action, cyber operations, strikes in third countries and relationships with armed groups. Violence was real, but both states often avoided sustained, openly acknowledged attacks from one homeland directly against the other.

COVERT

Sabotage and assassinations

Iran blamed Israel for attacks on nuclear-related facilities and killings of nuclear scientists. Israel often did not publicly claim specific operations.

SYRIA

Strikes outside Iran

Israel repeatedly struck Iranian-linked forces, weapons transfers and infrastructure in Syria, trying to prevent a stronger Iranian military presence near Israel.

PARTNERS

Conflict through armed groups

Iran supported Hezbollah and other organizations that could threaten Israel without every clash becoming a direct Iran-Israel state-to-state attack.

Step 3 · Watch the threshold move

The regional war creates new paths to direct attack.

The October 7, 2023 Hamas attack on Israel and the Gaza war intensified conflict involving Iran-supported groups across the region. Then 2024 brought a major change: Iran and Israel attacked one another directly.

APR 1, 2024

Strike on Iranian diplomatic compound in Damascus

An Israeli strike, according to widely reported accounts, kills senior IRGC officers at an Iranian diplomatic compound in Syria. Iran promises retaliation.

APR 13–14

Iran attacks Israel directly

Iran launches a large drone-and-missile attack from Iranian territory toward Israel—the first such direct attack by Iran on Israel.

OCT 1

Iran launches another direct missile attack

Iran fires a large ballistic-missile salvo toward Israel after escalating regional killings and fighting.

OCT 26

Israel strikes military targets in Iran

Israel carries out its largest acknowledged direct attack on Iran to that point, targeting air defenses and missile-related sites.

Step 4 · Return to today

Direct exchange becomes sustained war.

Once direct attacks had happened, later leaders were making decisions in a different environment. The next escalations were larger, longer and more openly aimed at Iranian military and nuclear capabilities.

2025

Israel launches a major war against Iran.

Israeli attacks hit Iranian nuclear and military targets and kill senior commanders and nuclear scientists. Iran responds with missiles and drones.

U.S.

American forces strike nuclear sites.

The United States attacks Iranian nuclear facilities during the 2025 war, moving direct U.S.-Iran military confrontation onto a new level.

FEB 2026

The present war begins.

On February 28, 2026, a new joint U.S.-Israeli offensive opens the current war and kills Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei. Iran retaliates across the region.

The key distinction

Trigger is not the same as cause.

February 28 is the immediate start date of the current war. That does not answer why leaders believed such an attack was necessary, possible or worth the risk.

Cumulative escalation

Each round changes the next decision.

After direct attack becomes thinkable, retaliation can create a new baseline. Earlier choices reshape the options later leaders believe they have.

Perspective check

Deterrence can work—until each side reads the other's restraint as weakness.

Both Iran and Israel have described attacks as efforts to prevent worse future threats. Reciprocal “deterrence” can also become a ladder upward.

Logic of restraint

A limited response can restore a boundary.

Leaders may attack just enough to show that an action carries costs while deliberately avoiding a wider war.

OR
Logic of escalation

A limited response can normalize the next round.

If each side concludes it survived the last exchange, direct attacks may become easier to authorize the next time.
