Core causal chain

Eight moments you should be able to connect.

You do not need every date in Iranian history. You do need the turning points that belong to this event's causal chain and the arrows that connect them.

1953

Mosaddegh coup

U.S.- and British-backed covert intervention helps remove Prime Minister Mohammad Mosaddegh after the oil nationalization crisis. The event becomes a durable symbol of foreign interference in Iranian political memory.

1979

Islamic Revolution + Hostage Crisis

The Shah falls, the Islamic Republic forms, the U.S.-Iran alliance collapses and the embassy hostage crisis creates a powerful American grievance.

1980–88

Iran-Iraq War

Iran survives invasion and eight years of war. The IRGC grows, while missiles, deterrence and influence beyond Iran become increasingly important to security strategy.

2015

JCPOA

Iran accepts nuclear limits and expanded verification in exchange for nuclear-related sanctions relief.

2018

U.S. leaves the JCPOA

Sanctions return; Iran later exceeds several deal limits; the nuclear dispute grows more dangerous.

2024

Direct Iran-Israel attacks

A shadow war crosses a major threshold as Iran attacks Israel directly and Israel strikes military targets inside Iran.

2025

Major Israel-Iran war + U.S. nuclear strikes

Direct conflict expands and U.S. forces strike Iranian nuclear facilities.

2026

Current war + Hormuz crisis

The February 28 offensive begins the current war. Iran's control and disruption of shipping around the Strait of Hormuz turns the conflict into a global energy and trade problem.

Vocabulary

Know these because they help you explain, not because they are trivia.

People & institutions

Mohammad Mosaddegh — prime minister associated with oil nationalization and removed in 1953.

Shah — Iran's monarch before the 1979 revolution.

Ayatollah — senior Shi'a religious title.

IRGC — Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, created after the revolution and central to Iran's security system.

Strategy & power

Deterrence — discouraging an action by making its expected cost too high.

Asymmetric strategy — using tools such as missiles, drones, irregular forces or geography rather than matching a stronger opponent system for system.

Proxy / partner force — an armed group supported by another state; the label does not automatically mean total control.

Sanctions — economic restrictions intended to pressure another actor.

Nuclear & geography

Enrichment — increasing the share of uranium-235 in uranium.

JCPOA — 2015 nuclear agreement that traded limits and monitoring for sanctions relief.

Chokepoint — narrow route through which unusually large amounts of trade must pass.

Strait of Hormuz — waterway connecting the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman.

Final argument prep

When did the 2026 Iran War really begin?

Your answer is not a date quiz. It is a ranked causal argument.

A strong response can do all five.

  1. Name one turning point as the most important explanatory beginning.
  2. Use at least three specific pieces of evidence from different points in the chain.
  3. Explain the causal mechanism connecting each piece of evidence to later events.
  4. Distinguish the literal start of the war—February 28, 2026—from the earlier condition you think best explains it.
  5. Address the strongest competing turning point rather than ignoring it.
Possible thesis frame

Long roots

“Although recent military decisions triggered the war, ____ is the strongest explanatory beginning because it changed ____ and shaped later decisions by ____.”

Possible thesis frame

Diplomatic break

“Earlier distrust mattered, but ____ deserves more weight because it removed/changed ____ and made later escalation more likely by ____.”

Possible thesis frame

Recent escalation

“The deeper history created tension, but war was not inevitable; ____ matters most because it crossed a threshold that ____.”
