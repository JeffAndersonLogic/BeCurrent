The eight-day investigation

The course path is not eight unrelated Iran topics.

Each day answers a question created by the day before it.

DAY 1

The War Now

Know the current decision-makers, Iran-aligned armed groups, Iran’s regional geography, and the Strait of Hormuz. Begin FRONTLINE Part I.

DAY 2

The Road to War

Finish FRONTLINE. End by asking which parts of the conflict cannot be explained by 2026 alone.

DAY 3

Why the U.S. and Iran Became Enemies

Reverse first to 1979, then farther back to 1953. Compare two turning points and two national memories.

DAY 4

Why Fight Far From Home?

Use the Iran-Iraq War to explain vulnerability, the IRGC, asymmetric strategy, and Iran’s armed network—without assuming every partner is controlled like a puppet.

DAY 5

The Nuclear Bargain

Understand what the JCPOA actually traded, what it did not solve, and how the post-2018 chain increased risk.

DAY 6

From Shadow War to Open War

Track how direct state-to-state attacks changed the escalation threshold and made later responses more thinkable.

DAY 7

The Hormuz Lever

Connect geography to the 1980s Tanker War and the 2026 collapse in shipping flows.

DAY 8

When Did This War Really Begin?

Rank causes, test the strongest rival explanation, and distinguish the literal outbreak from the explanatory beginning.

Core causal timeline

These are the historical stops you should be able to connect.

You do not need every date in Iranian history. You do need the turning points that belong to this conflict’s chain.

1953

Mosaddegh coup

Oil nationalization, domestic political conflict, and U.S.-British covert intervention become part of Iranian memory about sovereignty and foreign power.

1979

Revolution + hostages

The Shah falls, the Islamic Republic forms, the U.S.-Iran alliance collapses, and the hostage crisis creates a durable American grievance.

1980–88

Iran-Iraq War

Invasion and isolation shape Iranian security thinking as the IRGC grows and deterrence becomes more important.

2015/18

JCPOA + withdrawal

Nuclear limits and monitoring are exchanged for sanctions relief; U.S. withdrawal later changes incentives and intensifies the dispute.

2024

Direct attacks

The Iran-Israel shadow war crosses a threshold as direct state-to-state attacks become an available response.

2025

Major direct war

Direct conflict expands and the nuclear dispute becomes increasingly tied to military action.

FEB 2026

Current war

The present war begins with direct U.S.-Israeli attacks and Iranian retaliation across the region.

HORMUZ

Global leverage

Disrupted shipping turns a regional military conflict into a global energy, trade, and diplomatic problem.

Vocabulary that does explanatory work

Know the term because it helps you explain the conflict.

People & institutions

Mohammad Mosaddegh — Iranian prime minister associated with oil nationalization and removed in 1953.

Shah — Iran’s monarch before the 1979 revolution.

IRGC — Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, central to the Islamic Republic’s security system.

Quds Force — IRGC organization responsible for many relationships with armed groups outside Iran.

Strategy & relationships

Deterrence — discouraging action by making the expected cost too high.

Asymmetric strategy — using missiles, drones, irregular tactics, geography, or armed partners rather than matching a stronger opponent system for system.

Proxy / armed partner — a group supported by another state; the label does not automatically mean total control.

Historical memory — how past events continue to shape political interpretation and public expectations.

Nuclear & geography

Enrichment — increasing the share of uranium-235 in uranium.

IAEA — international agency that monitors nuclear programs and verifies commitments.

JCPOA — 2015 nuclear agreement trading nuclear limits and monitoring for nuclear-related sanctions relief.

Strait of Hormuz — chokepoint connecting the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman.

Final argument prep

When did the 2026 Iran War really begin?

Your answer is not a date quiz. It is a ranked causal argument.

A strong response can do all five.

  1. Name one turning point as the most important explanatory beginning.
  2. Connect evidence from several points in the chain through explicit causal mechanisms.
  3. Use at least three specific pieces of evidence from different parts of the unit.
  4. Distinguish the literal start of the war from the earlier condition you think best explains it.
  5. State the strongest case for a competing turning point before showing why yours carries more weight.
Long roots

1953 / 1979

Argue that durable mistrust and reciprocal historical memory shaped later crises.

Policy break

2018

Argue that losing the nuclear bargain substantially narrowed diplomatic options and increased coercive pressure.

Recent threshold

2024–26

Argue that deeper history created tension but direct-war precedents turned tension into an available military pathway.
