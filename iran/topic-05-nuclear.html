Iran at War · Topic 5 · Build the chain · 1957–2018

The Nuclear Bargain

Today's argument over Iranian uranium enrichment did not begin as a simple race toward a bomb. Iran's nuclear program began when the Shah was a U.S. ally, then became the center of sanctions, inspections, diplomacy and eventually military attacks.

Essential question

Did the collapse of the 2015 nuclear agreement make eventual war substantially more likely?

You need to understand what the deal did before you can judge what changed when the United States left it.

Reverse reveal
Who helped Iran start a nuclear program? The United States.
Step 2 · Uncover the nuclear root

The original nuclear relationship was cooperation.

Under the Shah, Iran and the United States cooperated on civilian nuclear technology as part of the Cold War-era Atoms for Peace program.

1957

Civil nuclear cooperation agreement

The United States and Iran sign an agreement for cooperation on peaceful uses of atomic energy.

1967

Tehran Research Reactor

A U.S.-supplied research reactor begins operating in Tehran. The program belongs to an Iran that Washington still sees as an ally.

1979

Revolution changes the political relationship

The Islamic Revolution breaks the alliance. Nuclear activity continues unevenly over the following decades under a very different government.

2002+

Undeclared facilities intensify international concern

Public revelations about facilities at Natanz and Arak increase scrutiny, inspections and disputes over whether Iran's nuclear program could support a weapons option.

Step 3 · Build the bargain

The JCPOA trades nuclear limits for sanctions relief.

The Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action was not a peace treaty and did not solve every U.S.-Iran dispute. It was a negotiated system for limiting and monitoring parts of Iran's nuclear program.

What the bargain actually did

Iran agreed to restrictions on uranium enrichment, centrifuges and its stockpile of enriched uranium, along with expanded International Atomic Energy Agency monitoring. In return, nuclear-related sanctions were to be lifted or suspended as Iran met its commitments.

One useful number is 3.67%: under the deal, Iran agreed not to enrich uranium above 3.67% U-235 at its declared enrichment facilities for the specified period. That is far below weapons-grade enrichment. The IAEA's job was verification, not deciding whether the agreement was politically wise.

Keep the scope straight: The JCPOA targeted the nuclear issue. Critics argued that it did not sufficiently address missiles, armed partners, regional conflict or restrictions that would eventually expire. Supporters argued that trying to solve every dispute in one agreement would have made a nuclear deal impossible.

Core tradeoff

Constraint for relief

Iran accepted temporary and long-term nuclear restrictions; the other parties offered sanctions relief and normalization of nuclear-related economic activity.

The question: Could a limited agreement survive while the broader relationship stayed hostile?

Before the break, be precise about the bargain.

The agreement addressed nuclear activity and verification. It deliberately left missiles, armed partners and the wider regional rivalry outside the deal. That limited scope is essential to evaluating both its strengths and its weaknesses.

Step 4 · Follow the break

The United States leaves the agreement.

President Donald Trump announced the end of U.S. participation on May 8, 2018 and the reimposition of sanctions. The decision changed the incentives holding the agreement together.

Withdrawal argument

The deal was too narrow and too temporary.

The Trump administration argued that the JCPOA did not permanently block Iran's nuclear path and failed to address missiles, regional armed groups and other Iranian behavior.

2018
Keep-the-deal argument

Verified limits were better than an unconstrained program.

Supporters argued that the agreement imposed inspections and measurable restrictions and that non-nuclear disputes could be addressed separately.

What happened after the withdrawal?

U.S. sanctions returned. Iran remained in the agreement for a period with the other participants but later exceeded several JCPOA limits as diplomacy failed to restore the original bargain. The nuclear dispute again became more acute, and concern about Iran's ability to enrich uranium to higher levels increased.

That is a causal chain, but not a single-cause explanation: withdrawal → restored sanctions → reduced incentives for Iranian compliance → expanding nuclear activity → greater fear and coercive pressure. At every arrow, governments still made choices.

Step 4 · Back toward war

Nuclear fear shifts from negotiation toward force.

By the mid-2020s, diplomacy repeatedly failed to produce a durable replacement. In 2025 Israel launched a war against Iran, and the United States struck Iranian nuclear sites. The nuclear issue remained central as the larger confrontation moved into the 2026 war.

2025

Military attacks reach nuclear sites.

The long-running debate over whether diplomacy could contain Iran's program increasingly includes direct military action.

2026

Nuclear policy remains in negotiations.

Even after major fighting paused, the nuclear program remained one of the unresolved questions in U.S.-Iran diplomacy.

Historical tension

More pressure can create leverage — or reduce trust.

Sanctions and military threats can raise the cost of a nuclear program. They can also increase the value Iranian leaders place on deterrence and reduce confidence in negotiated promises.
