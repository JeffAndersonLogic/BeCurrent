Topic 8 · Unit review and final argument

Back to the Headline

Topic 1 showed the war without its history. Topic 8 returns to the war with that history. Today you will watch this week’s main news story about Iran, connect it to the eight turning points in this unit, and answer the unit question.

Unit question

Was the 2026 Iran War mainly the result of recent decisions, or decades of unresolved conflict?

Your grade depends on your reasons, not on the side you choose. You may answer “both,” but you must say which one explains more.

The rule
A date is not a cause by itself. To call an event a cause, explain how it led to what came next.
Step 1 · This week's story

The war this week

Watch two news reports. The first report covers a peace plan from Iran. The second report covers President Trump’s rejection of that plan. Then read the summary below and look for events from earlier in the unit.

What was reported · NPR · September 26, 2026

Trump rejects plan to reopen Strait of Hormuz

  • At the United Nations, Iran offered a “concrete seven-day plan” to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and restart negotiations with the United States, if the U.S. met its conditions.
  • President Trump rejected it. “They want to make a deal where they open the strait immediately because they’re losing so badly,” he said.
  • The plan is similar to an agreement from June. Under the June deal, the U.S. eased its naval blockade and sanctions and released frozen Iranian money. Iran promised not to seek a nuclear weapon and to reopen the strait. The deal collapsed because some of its wording was unclear, including who would control the strait. Then the strikes started again.
  • Iran’s president told CBS that the U.S. must show “that it does not intend to overthrow our government.” He said Iran would allow U.N. nuclear inspectors into the country if the plan is accepted.

Connect the story to the unit

This week’s story connects to most topics in this unit.

Each card below takes one detail from the story and names the topic that explains it. Read the cards. No writing is needed for this part.

Reopen Hormuz

Iran is using the strait to bargain.

Iran offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. In return, Iran wants the U.S. blockade and sanctions to end. Topic 7 called this diplomatic leverage.

Topic 7 · The Hormuz Lever
Sanctions and inspectors

This is the same trade as the 2015 deal.

Iran would accept nuclear inspections. In return, the pressure on Iran would ease. The 2015 nuclear deal (the JCPOA) used the same trade.

Topic 5 · The Nuclear Bargain
“Does not intend to overthrow our government”

Iran’s president is referring to 1953.

In 1953, the U.S. and Britain helped remove Iran’s prime minister. Iran’s president wants a promise that the U.S. will not try to remove Iran’s government again.

Topic 3 · 1953 and 1979
The June deal collapsed

Another agreement failed.

Each side expects the other side to break its promises. This distrust grew each time an agreement failed, including when the U.S. left the nuclear deal in 2018.

Topics 3 and 5 · Distrust
Including Lebanon

The war involves more than two countries.

CBS News reported that Iran’s plan also asked for an end to fighting across the region. That includes Lebanon, where Hezbollah, an armed group backed by Iran, operates.

Topic 4 · Why Fight Far From Home?
The strikes started again

Direct attacks are now expected.

Before 2024, Iran and Israel avoided attacking each other directly. After the attacks of 2024 and 2025, direct strikes became a normal part of the conflict.

Topic 6 · From Shadow War to Open War

Step 2 · Sort the eight cards

Long-running conflict or recent decision?

Each of these eight events helped cause the war. Sort the cards on this page. Then choose the three events that explain the war best.

Three steps

  1. Sort. On each card, click one group. Long-running conflict means a fear or grievance that lasted for decades. Recent decision means a choice leaders made in the last few years that could have gone differently. Some cards fit both groups. Choose the group that fits the event best.
  2. Choose. Click Top three on the three events that best explain this week’s story. The first card you click is #1, the second is #2, and the third is #3.
  3. Check. Look at the summary below the cards. An older event is not automatically more important. A newer event is not automatically more important. Then click Start Filing 1 with my picks.
1953

Coup removes Mosaddegh

The U.S. and Britain helped remove Iran’s prime minister. Many Iranians remember this as foreign powers taking control of their government.

1979

Revolution and hostage crisis

Iran and the U.S. went from allies to enemies. Each country remembers the other as the side that started the conflict.

1980–88

Iran-Iraq War

Iran survived an eight-year invasion by Iraq. Afterward, Iran built missiles, a stronger Revolutionary Guard, and armed partner groups in other countries for protection.

2015

The nuclear deal

Iran accepted limits and inspections on its nuclear program. In return, many sanctions were lifted. The deal showed that an agreement was possible.

2018

U.S. leaves the deal

The U.S. left the nuclear deal and brought back sanctions. Iran later went past the deal’s nuclear limits. Trust between the two countries fell further.

2024

First direct attacks

Iran and Israel attacked each other’s territory directly for the first time. The hidden conflict between them became open fighting.

2025

A bigger war

Israel and Iran fought a longer war. The U.S. bombed Iranian nuclear sites.

FEB 2026

The current war begins

The U.S. and Israel launched a joint attack on Iran on February 28, 2026. Iran struck back, and shipping through the Strait of Hormuz nearly stopped.

Step 3 · Decide

Write one paragraph. Use the sentence frame below to organize it.

VERSUS
What explains it

An earlier turning point

A turning point answers a different question: “What earlier event made this war more likely?”

Counterarguments

Use one of these as the other side of your argument.

A counterargument is a reason someone might disagree with you. A strong argument explains the best counterargument and then answers it.

LONG-TERM

Old distrust

Trust between Iran and the U.S. broke down after 1953 and 1979. Without that distrust, later nuclear and military crises might have been easier to solve.

2015 TO 2018

The broken deal

The 2015 deal showed that war could be avoided. For that reason, the end of the deal in 2018 may matter more than older events.

2024 TO 2026

Recent attacks

Leaders made choices from 2024 to 2026 that earlier leaders had avoided, such as direct attacks. Those choices may explain the war better than older history.
