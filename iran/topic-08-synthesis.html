Topic 8 · Closing the loop

Back to the Headline

On the first day of this unit you watched the war without its history. Today you watch this week’s biggest Iran story with eight turning points behind you. Then you decide how much of this war comes from recent decisions and how much comes from decades of unresolved conflict.

Unit question

Was the 2026 Iran War mainly the result of recent decisions, or decades of unresolved conflict?

You are graded on your reasons, not on which side you pick. “Both” is allowed if you say which one does more of the work.

The rule
A date becomes a cause only when you can explain the mechanism.
Step 1 · This week's story

Start where the unit started: the war now.

Watch two reports: the peace plan Iran offered this week, and President Trump’s answer. Then read what was reported, this time looking for the history underneath it.

What was reported · NPR · September 26, 2026

Trump rejects plan to reopen Strait of Hormuz

  • At the United Nations, Iran offered a “concrete seven-day plan” to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and restart negotiations with the United States, if the U.S. met its conditions.
  • President Trump rejected it. “They want to make a deal where they open the strait immediately because they’re losing so badly,” he said.
  • The plan is similar to an agreement from June. Under that deal the U.S. eased its naval blockade and sanctions and freed frozen Iranian money, and Iran promised not to seek a nuclear weapon and to reopen the strait. It collapsed over vague wording, including who would control the strait, and the strikes started again.
  • Iran’s president told CBS the U.S. must show “that it does not intend to overthrow our government,” and said Iran would let U.N. nuclear inspectors in if the plan is accepted.

Almost every topic in this unit is inside this one story.

Each card takes one piece of the story and points to the topic that explains it. No writing yet. Just notice.

Reopen Hormuz

The strait is a bargaining chip.

Iran is offering open water in exchange for relief from the blockade and sanctions. That is the diplomatic lever from last class.

Topic 7 · The Hormuz Lever
Sanctions and inspectors

It is the 2015 trade again.

Pressure relief in exchange for a nuclear promise and inspections is the same basic bargain as the JCPOA.

Topic 5 · The Nuclear Bargain
“Does not intend to overthrow our government”

Iran’s president is talking about 1953.

He wants a promise that the U.S. will not try to remove Iran’s government. Iranians remember the last time it happened.

Topic 3 · 1953 and 1979
The June deal collapsed

A deal was made, then fell apart. Again.

Each side expects the other to break its word. That distrust grew every time a deal failed, including when the U.S. left the nuclear deal in 2018.

Topics 3 and 5 · Distrust
Including Lebanon

The war is bigger than two countries.

CBS reported that Iran’s plan also asked for an end to fighting across the region, including Lebanon, where its armed partner Hezbollah operates.

Topic 4 · Why Fight Far From Home?
The strikes started again

Direct strikes are now a normal option.

After the direct attacks of 2024 and 2025, bombing Iran or Israel is no longer unthinkable. That changes what leaders are bargaining over.

1953

Coup removes Mosaddegh

The U.S. and Britain help overthrow Iran’s prime minister. Iranians remember foreign powers taking away their government.

1979

Revolution and hostage crisis

Allies become enemies. Each side builds a memory of the other as the aggressor.

1980–88

Iran-Iraq War

Iran survives an eight-year invasion and builds missiles, the IRGC and armed partners abroad so it is never that exposed again.

2015

The nuclear deal

Nuclear limits and inspections are traded for sanctions relief. A deal turns out to be possible.

2018

U.S. leaves the deal

Sanctions return. Iran later exceeds the nuclear limits, and trust drops further.

2024

First direct attacks

Iran and Israel strike each other’s territory. The shadow war becomes open, state-to-state fighting.

2025

A bigger war

Israel and Iran fight a sustained war, and the U.S. bombs Iranian nuclear sites.

FEB 2026

The current war begins

A joint U.S.-Israeli offensive starts the present war. Iran retaliates and shipping through Hormuz collapses.

DEEP

Long roots

Without the collapse of trust after 1953 and 1979, later nuclear and military crises might have been easier to manage.

MID

The broken deal

The 2015 deal showed war was not inevitable, so the 2018 breakdown may matter more than older grievances.

NEAR

Recent escalation

Whatever the older history, choices in 2024 to 2026 crossed lines that earlier leaders had managed to avoid.
