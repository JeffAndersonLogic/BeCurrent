Iran’s Armed Partners

What does “Iranian proxy” mean?

The word is useful, but it can also make complicated relationships look more controlled than they really are.

Working definition: a proxy is an armed group supported by a state with money, weapons, training, intelligence, or political backing that can advance the state’s interests without the state directly conducting every operation. Important: these groups have their own leaders, constituencies, and goals. “Iran-backed” or “armed partner” is often more precise than assuming Tehran controls every decision.
Lebanon

Hezbollah

A Shiite political party and armed movement built with major Iranian support. Historically Iran’s closest and most capable regional armed partner, though heavily weakened by recent fighting.

CLOSEST STRATEGIC PARTNER
Yemen

Houthis / Ansar Allah

An Iran-aligned movement that controls much of Yemen. Iran supplies weapons, training, and intelligence, but experts often describe the Houthis as an informal partner with meaningful autonomy.

IRAN-ALIGNED PARTNER
Iraq

Iran-aligned militias

A network rather than one organization. Groups including Kata’ib Hezbollah have received IRGC support and can pressure U.S. forces and Iranian rivals from Iraqi territory.

NETWORK OF MILITIAS
Palestinian territories

Hamas & Palestinian Islamic Jihad

Iran has provided military and financial support, but these Palestinian groups have their own political aims and command structures. Their relationship with Tehran is not identical to Hezbollah’s.

Points of Understanding

Keep these four ideas in mind as you watch.

You do not need to memorize every name or detail. These are the ideas that will make the documentary easier to follow.

1

Iran is a regional power.

Its location, population, military capacity, and relationships with armed groups give it influence well beyond its borders.

2

Iran’s leaders have different roles.

The president matters, but the Supreme Leader and security institutions hold major authority over national security and military strategy.

3

Iran-backed does not mean Iran-controlled.

Hezbollah, the Houthis, Iraqi militias, Hamas, and Palestinian Islamic Jihad have different relationships with Tehran and different degrees of independence.

4

Geography changes the stakes.

The Strait of Hormuz gives events around Iran consequences for shipping, energy markets, and countries far beyond the Middle East.

Language

Proxy ≠ puppet.

Iran can provide weapons, money, training, intelligence, or political support to an armed group without controlling every decision that group makes. In this unit, Iran-backed, Iran-aligned, and armed partner are often more precise than assuming direct command.

Use the evidence, not the label: ask what support is documented, what interests the group has of its own, and how much operational control Iran can actually be shown to exercise.
