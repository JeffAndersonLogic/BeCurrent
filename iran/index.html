Iran at War

The major air campaign has ended, but the argument over peace has not. Iran, the United States and Israel reached this point through decades of changing alliances, revolution, nuclear diplomacy, sanctions and regional conflict.

Teacher view: Student target: distinguish immediate triggers from deeper causes. The historical spine is deliberately narrow: U.S.–Iran trust, the nuclear dispute and regional power. Avoid treating 1953 as a single-cause explanation.
What happened now

From air war to pressure campaign

Joint U.S.-Israeli attacks on Iran began February 28, 2026. A ceasefire took hold in April after 38 days of major U.S. combat operations. A June interim agreement then broke down over the Strait of Hormuz, sanctions, ports and the terms for a larger settlement. By late August, the sides were not exchanging sustained fire, but peace talks had stalled and Washington had turned again to economic pressure.

2018

The United States leaves the nuclear deal.

President Donald Trump ended U.S. participation in the JCPOA on May 8, 2018 and began reimposing sanctions that had been lifted under the agreement. The administration argued that the deal did not adequately address Iran's broader behavior or permanently block a nuclear pathway. The decision did not erase the agreement for the other participants, but it changed the diplomatic environment and restored U.S. economic pressure.

Read the archived White House explanation →

2015

A bargain tries to contain the nuclear dispute.

Iran and the P5+1 — China, France, Germany, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States — reached the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action on July 14, 2015. Iran accepted limits and monitoring on parts of its nuclear program in exchange for nuclear-related sanctions relief. The IAEA later verified steps required for implementation.

1979

The ally becomes the enemy.

Iran's revolution removed Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, a close U.S. partner, and created an Islamic Republic led by Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini. Later that year, Iranian students seized the U.S. Embassy in Tehran. More than 50 Americans were held for 444 days. The crisis hardened public attitudes on both sides and the United States broke diplomatic relations with Iran in 1980.

“The demonstrators are demanding the extradition of the Shah.”

1953

A covert intervention becomes part of the relationship's memory.

Prime Minister Mohammad Mosaddegh had nationalized Iran's oil industry and was locked in a political struggle with the Shah. In August 1953, a covert operation backed by the United States and Britain helped remove Mosaddegh and strengthen the Shah. Declassified U.S. records document CIA involvement. The coup did not make the 1979 revolution inevitable, but it became an enduring symbol of foreign interference in Iranian political memory.

Declassified CIA memorandum · March 8, 1954
“The ouster of Mossadeq was successfully accomplished on 19 Aug 1953.”
Directorate of Plans memorandum, published by the U.S. Office of the Historian.

Verification

What is independently observable?

Shipping traffic, public sanctions, official agreements and military actions can be cross-checked. Claims about motives require more caution.
