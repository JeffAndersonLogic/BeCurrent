Topic 4 · Reverse History stop · 1980–1988

Why Fight Far From Home?

In 2026, Iran's confrontation with Israel and the United States includes direct state-to-state attacks while Iran-aligned armed groups remain active across the region. To understand why Iran built a strategy of missiles and armed partnerships beyond its borders, reverse to 1980, when Iraq invaded a newly revolutionary Iran.

Today's question

To what extent is Iran's regional military strategy best understood as deterrence, power projection, or both, and how much of that strategy can be traced to the Iran-Iraq War?

A strategy can be defensive in the eyes of the state using it and threatening in the eyes of the states facing it.

Reverse move
What could make a government want threats stopped before they reach its border?
Strategy toolkit

Three terms you will need.

Use these precisely. They describe different parts of the same strategy.

Deterrence

Make attack look too costly.

Make an opponent believe attacking you will cost too much.

Asymmetric strategy

Do not fight only on their terms.

Avoid fighting a stronger opponent where that opponent is strongest.

Power projection

Reach beyond your borders.

Use military force or influence beyond your own borders.

Step 1 · September 1980

Iraq invades a revolutionary Iran.

The Islamic Republic was less than two years old when Saddam Hussein's Iraq attacked. The war lasted until 1988 and became one of the formative security experiences of the new regime.

1980

Cross-border invasion

Iraqi forces attack along a broad front, expecting to exploit revolutionary disorder and settle territorial and political disputes.

8 YRS

A war of attrition

Iran survives the opening assault and recovers much lost territory, but the conflict continues with enormous human and economic costs.

MISSILES + CHEM

Attacks expose vulnerability

Iraq strikes Iranian cities with ballistic missiles and uses chemical weapons during the war. Iran begins acquiring ballistic missiles of its own while arguing that it cannot depend on outside protection.

1988

A ceasefire, not a clean victory

Iran accepts U.N. Security Council Resolution 598 and the fighting ends without the decisive revolutionary victory Tehran had once sought.

Step 2 · The institution

The Revolutionary Guard grows through the war.

The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, or IRGC, was created after the 1979 revolution to defend the new political system. The Iran-Iraq War transformed it into a larger and more capable military institution.

Two military systems, one state.

Iran retained its regular armed forces, but the revolutionary government also developed the IRGC as a force loyal to the new system. Over time the Guard acquired ground, air, missile, naval and external-operation roles. Over time, the Quds Force became the main IRGC organization responsible for relationships with armed groups outside Iran.

During the Iran-Iraq War, Iran began building ballistic-missile capability and gained experience with unconventional ways of confronting stronger opponents. Over the following decades, Iran expanded its missile forces, drones and regional armed partnerships rather than trying to match stronger rivals weapon for weapon. Analysts describe this broader approach as asymmetric strategy: raise the cost of attacking Iran without fighting a stronger opponent only where that opponent is strongest.

Important distinction: The Iran-Iraq War did not invent every part of this strategy. Revolutionary ideology, Lebanon's civil war, Israel's 1982 invasion of Lebanon, later U.S. military deployments and regional competition also mattered. The war is one major security experience that helps explain why deterrence became so important.
Evidence station

Test the causal story with three pieces of evidence.

The war matters, but it cannot explain everything.

Evidence A · Missiles

Vulnerability produces a response.

Iraqi ballistic-missile attacks exposed a major Iranian weakness. During the war, Iran began acquiring ballistic missiles of its own, an early step toward a capability that later became central to Iranian deterrence.

Source: U.S. Institute of Peace, Iran Primer

Evidence B · Chemical warfare

Extreme threats reach Iranian territory.

United Nations missions investigated chemical-weapons use during the conflict, and the Security Council condemned the continuing use of chemical weapons in 1988. The experience reinforced Iran's sense of wartime vulnerability.

Source: United Nations, 1988

Evidence C · Hezbollah

The war is not the whole explanation.

Iranian support and IRGC training were important to Hezbollah's development in Lebanon in the early 1980s. But Lebanon's civil war and Israel's 1982 invasion were also crucial. This relationship shows both Iranian strategy and the limits of a one-cause explanation.

Source: Council on Foreign Relations

Step 3 · Strategic depth

Iran builds influence beyond Iran.

Beginning in the 1980s and expanding over decades, the IRGC developed relationships with armed organizations across the region. These groups are not all identical, and “proxy” can overstate how directly Tehran controls every action.

Lebanon

Hezbollah

Iranian support was important to Hezbollah's development in the early 1980s amid Lebanon's civil war and Israel's invasion. Hezbollah became Iran's most powerful regional partner.

Iraq, Syria, Yemen and Palestinian territories

A wider network

Later relationships connected Iran to armed groups and political actors across several conflicts. The strength, goals and degree of Iranian control differ from group to group.

Strategic effect

Push the confrontation outward

Partner forces can threaten adversaries far from Iranian territory, complicate retaliation and expand Iranian influence without requiring Iran's conventional forces to occupy another country.

2026 update: the network is not static. Hezbollah was considerably weakened during the fighting that intensified in 2024, but it remains capable of military action. The Houthis remain an important Iran-aligned armed force in Yemen. At the same time, Iran's confrontation with Israel and the United States now includes direct state-to-state attacks, so regional partners are only one part of the current conflict.

Perspective check

Deterrence to one side can look like encirclement to another.

This is a genuine security dilemma: one state's attempt to feel safer can cause its rivals to feel less safe.

Iranian security frame

Create costs before war reaches Iran.

Missiles, armed partners and influence in nearby states can make a stronger adversary hesitate before attacking Iran directly.

SECURITY DILEMMA
U.S. / Israeli / Gulf rival frame
