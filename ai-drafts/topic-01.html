What AI Actually Is

A very good guesser · Skill: Generalizing from Evidence

Today's story

You've probably used artificial intelligence already this week. Your feed decides what to show. Your email moves suspicious messages into spam. Your phone predicts what you meant to type. How can a computer do these things? Today we look inside the idea behind many AI systems: learning patterns from examples. Then we test what a chatbot can and cannot know.

Unit question: AI is already making decisions that affect your life. Which decisions should a person still make, and who gets to set the rules?

Learning targets: Explain how some AI systems learn patterns; distinguish AI from ordinary programmed tools; explain why a convincing answer can still be wrong.

Success: Use two examples, explain a prediction, and support a conclusion with evidence from today's activities.

Chapter 1

You already use it

A video feed ranks posts, autocorrect suggests words, and a spam filter sorts email. These tools are not identical, but they can use patterns learned from data. Some everyday features use AI, while others simply follow fixed instructions.

Read the background

Artificial intelligence is a broad term for computer systems that perform tasks associated with abilities such as recognizing patterns, understanding language, or making predictions. Not all AI works the same way, and not all automation is AI.

Chapter 2 · Paper first

Be the Model

On paper, complete these five starters before asking a chatbot: Peanut butter and ___; The capital of France is ___; Once upon a ___; My favorite food is ___; The best movie ever is ___.

Which have a predictable answer? Which depend on the person? Compare the pattern, not just the words.

Read the background

A prediction is an estimate of what is likely to come next. When many examples have the same pattern, guessing can be easier. When the question asks for a personal preference, there may be no single correct completion.

Chapter 3

Rules versus learning

A simple calculator follows mathematical instructions. A machine-learning spam filter can learn patterns from examples of messages marked as spam and not spam. The important question is how the system decides, not whether it runs on a computer.

Read the background

Training data means examples used to develop a system. A pattern is something that appears repeatedly. Some AI is trained on examples; other AI approaches work differently. A feature name alone does not always reveal how it was built.

Read the background

A chatbot based on a large language model learns statistical relationships in enormous amounts of text and generates responses in steps. Calling it a 'next-word predictor' is a useful starting analogy, but modern models can also reason through problems, use tools, and retrieve information. A fluent answer is not proof that it is accurate.

Chapter 5

Why this matters now

Students and adults increasingly use chatbots for studying, writing, and finding information. A useful tool can also give an incorrect answer with convincing wording. Tomorrow's question: How do we check when the machine sounds sure?
