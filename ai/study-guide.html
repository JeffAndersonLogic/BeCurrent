AI Study Guide

The test and answer key are in Canvas, not on BeCurrent. This guide prepares the reasoning, vocabulary, and source-checking skills, not the individual test items.

What to understand

AI1

Artificial intelligence, training data, pattern, prediction, chatbot, large language model; fixed instructions versus learned patterns; fluency versus truth.

AI2

Hallucination, bias, deepfake, corroboration; independently verify a court citation; administrative fine versus criminal verdict.

AI3

Automation, task, employment gap, data center, tradeoff, moratorium; compare Stanford and Yale scopes; distinguish Meta promises and verified outcomes.

AI4

Regulation, federalism, disclosure, terms of service, policy; school and company rules; limits of executive orders; designing enforceable rules.

Practice reasoning in new situations

Source check: An AI supplies a very specific quotation. What independent record would you open first?

Tradeoff: A data center promises construction jobs, but local officials worry about infrastructure. Which claims are promises and which are observed outcomes?

Policy: A student uses AI to test their own understanding. Under what conditions would that help learning?

Exam approach: read the entire question, identify what evidence it gives, reject statements that claim more certainty than the evidence, then choose the best supported answer.

Remember: Your opinion on AI is not graded. We assess your evidence, distinction between claims and facts, causal reasoning, and ability to recognize uncertainty.
