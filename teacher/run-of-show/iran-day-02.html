Thursday, Sept. 10 · Documentary Bridge

Finish the Current Story

Purpose: complete FRONTLINE while shifting students from “what happened?” toward “what requires historical explanation?” The film remains the dominant cognitive load.

Non-negotiable takeaway

Recent events explain triggers. History explains deeper conditions.

Students should leave with a backward-looking question, not a pile of notes.

Before class

Resume cleanly

  • Queue FRONTLINE where Day 1 stopped
  • Open Topic 2 student page
  • Have the retrieval screen visible at the bell
  • Keep captions on
Cognitive load

Film first

  • 3-minute retrieval only
  • No note packet
  • Three listening categories: recent, older, interpretation
  • One written bridge question at the end
Watch for

Likely misconceptions

  • Documentary = neutral transcript
  • Most recent event = deepest cause
  • Any older grievance automatically “caused” 2026
  • Source criticism means dismissing the source
Suggested block flow

Run of Show

0–4

Retrieval, not reteaching

Pairs recover four people, Hormuz, and two Iran-aligned groups. Add one verbal check: “Proxy does not necessarily mean what?” Answer: puppet / total control.

4–7

Frame the watch

Give the single question from the lesson: “Why do Iran, Israel, and the United States distrust one another this much?” Tell students to sort explanations they hear into trigger, escalation, or deeper condition.

7–66

FRONTLINE Part II

Resume the documentary. Pause only when a person/place/relationship becomes a barrier to understanding. Avoid turning every important moment into a mini-lecture.

Three listening cues: Which decisions happened in 2024–26? Which grievances predate current leaders? Where does the film make an argument rather than simply report an event?

66–72

Source check

Ask: “What can a documentary do well that a single source cannot?” Then: “What does it still require us to judge?” Land on causal weight and interpretation.

72–78

Make the Reverse visible

Project or read the backward chain: 2026 → 1979 → 1953. Emphasize that BeCurrent is not theme-matching; students are tracing the actual history of this named conflict backward.

Final 5

Protect the bridge question

Students answer: “After FRONTLINE, what is the most important historical question you need answered before you can explain the 2026 war?”

IEP / 504 reduction

What to reduce

  • Oral retrieval can substitute for written retrieval.
  • Students only need one backward-looking question in writing.
  • Do not require detailed film notes.
  • Allow a sentence stem: “Before I can explain 2026, I need to understand why/how…”
If the film runs long

Cut the source-check discussion first

The existing lesson already identifies this priority. Protect the final historical question because it is the bridge into Reverse History.

Transition to Day 3

Set up the comparison

“Next class we test two turning points: 1979 and 1953. Your job will be to decide which changed the U.S.–Iran relationship more—and why.”
