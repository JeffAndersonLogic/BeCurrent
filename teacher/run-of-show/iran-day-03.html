Monday, Sept. 14 · Reverse History

1953 + 1979

Purpose: trace the current U.S.–Iran relationship backward through two turning points and make students weigh causal importance rather than memorize chronology.

Non-negotiable takeaway

1953 and 1979 created different historical memories.

Students should understand reciprocal grievance without collapsing the lesson into “1953 caused everything” or “1979 came from nowhere.”

Before class

Stage the comparison

  • Open Topic 3 student page
  • Queue PBS Operation Ajax clip (6:08)
  • Optional Taken Hostage clip available
  • Display the question: “Which transformed U.S.-Iran relations more: 1953 or 1979?”
Cognitive load

Mechanism over detail

  • Effects first, causes second
  • Use only enough revolutionary detail to explain the rupture
  • Use 1953 to explain political memory and intervention
  • Protect the final comparison
Watch for

Likely misconceptions

  • The CIA single-handedly caused the revolution
  • All Iranians shared one motive in 1979
  • The hostage crisis and revolution were the same event
  • Explaining grievance means endorsing later actions
Suggested block flow

Run of Show

0–6

Reopen the 2026 question

Start with distrust, not dates: “Why do these governments interpret one another through such deep suspicion?” Then reveal today’s comparison question.

6–22

Reverse stop 1: 1979

Teach the minimum causal chain: Shah as U.S. ally → broad revolution → Islamic Republic → hostage crisis → durable American political memory. Emphasize that the revolution had multiple opposition groups and motives.

22–29

Optional visual support on hostages

If students need the human/political weight of the hostage crisis, use the PBS Taken Hostage clip. If discussion is already productive, skip it.

29–36

Operation Ajax clip

Show the required 6:08 PBS American Experience clip. Frame it with one question: “What did foreign intervention make later Iranian critics believe about the United States?”

36–52

Reverse stop 2: 1953

Teach Mosaddegh, oil nationalization, the U.S.-British covert operation, and the strengthened Shah. Keep the source-discipline line explicit: intervention is documented; total control over all later Iranian outcomes is not.

52–62

Evidence station

Use the declassified U.S. record to separate what the source can prove from what it cannot. Ask students to identify one supported claim and one overclaim.

62–72

Two memories

Place Iranian memory of 1953 beside American memory of 1979–81. Ask: “How can different historical memories cause each side to interpret later actions as proof of what it already fears?”

Final 10

Protect the Claim Builder

Students choose which year transformed the relationship more, explain why, and concede what the other year explains better. This is causal weighting, not blame assignment.

IEP / 504 reduction

What to reduce

  • Provide the two causal chains verbally before writing.
  • Allow sentence frames for each filing.
  • Skip the optional hostage clip before cutting evidence or the final claim.
  • Accept concise responses that show mechanism.
If time runs short

Cut in this order

  1. Optional Taken Hostage clip
  2. Extended biography/background
  3. Extra discussion after source limits

Do not cut the 1953/1979 comparison or final claim.

Teacher language

Keep causation disciplined

Use: “1953 helps explain later Iranian distrust.” Avoid: “1953 caused the 2026 war.” Use: “1979 transformed the relationship.” Avoid: “Iran became anti-American for one simple reason.”
