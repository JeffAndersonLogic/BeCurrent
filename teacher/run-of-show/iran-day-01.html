Tuesday, Sept. 8 · Current Anchor

The War Now

Purpose: orient students to the people, armed partners, geography, and core vocabulary they need before FRONTLINE becomes the main source of the day. Keep reading and writing deliberately light.

Non-negotiable takeaway

Students should leave able to follow the conflict.

They do not need a finished explanation yet. They need to know who is acting, where Iran sits, what Hormuz is, and why “Iran-backed” does not automatically mean “Iran-controlled.”

Before class

Open and stage

  • Student lesson: Iran Topic 1
  • FRONTLINE queued at the beginning
  • Project regional map before discussion
  • Check audio and captions
Cognitive load

Protect comprehension

  • No note packet
  • One exit response only
  • Use visuals before explanation
  • Pause film only when confusion blocks understanding
Watch for

Likely misconceptions

  • Iranian president = highest authority
  • Proxy = puppet
  • Hormuz is “inside” Iran
  • All Iran-aligned groups have identical goals
Suggested block flow

Run of Show

0–5

Open with the unit question

Project: “Was the 2026 Iran War mainly the result of recent decisions, or decades of unresolved conflict?” Tell students they are not answering it today. Today is about building the map needed to understand the question.

5–15

Key figures

Move quickly through Trump, Netanyahu, Mojtaba Khamenei, and Pezeshkian. Emphasize roles, not biographies. Ask one check: “Who has the most authority inside Iran on security questions?”

15–23

Iran-backed armed partners

Define proxy, then immediately complicate it: support does not equal total control. Use Hezbollah, Houthis, Iraqi militias, Hamas/PIJ only as examples of different relationships.

23–30

Geography + Hormuz

Locate Iran, Iraq, Saudi Arabia, Israel, Turkey, the Persian Gulf, and the Arabian Peninsula. Then zoom to Hormuz. Ask: “Why can a narrow waterway make a regional war matter globally?”

30–80

FRONTLINE Part I

Let the film carry the class. Give only three listening cues: who claims to be acting defensively, where Iran-backed groups change the balance, and what makes students ask “How did we get here?”

Viewer note: FRONTLINE warns of graphic imagery. Use discretion if a scene is likely to overwhelm the instructional purpose.

Final 5

Protect the exit question

Students answer: “What relationship, person, or location from today most needs a historical explanation?” Do not turn this into a summary.

IEP / 504 reduction

What to reduce

  • Do not require copied definitions.
  • Let students rely on the person cards and maps visually.
  • Accept a single-sentence exit question.
  • Use captions on the documentary.
If time runs short

Cut in this order

  1. Extended discussion of individual armed groups
  2. Extra statistics on Hormuz
  3. Any additional note-taking

Do not cut the map, proxy distinction, or exit question.

Transition to Day 2

Leave them unfinished

“Today gave you the pieces. Next class we finish the current story and start deciding which parts of it cannot be explained without going backward.”
