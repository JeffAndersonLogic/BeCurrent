TEACHER RUN OF SHOW / AI4 / 2026-12-02

Who Makes the Rules?

Which decisions should a person still make, and who gets to set the rules?

Class opening

Should students be allowed to use AI to brainstorm homework? What about handing in an essay AI wrote?

Timing / 55-minute before-lunch route

0–4 min opening; 4–15 min read and orient; 15–29 min evidence investigation; 29–40 min practice and answers; 40–50 min checkpoint/review; 50–55 min Canvas filing.

Learning objective

  • I can identify who makes rules in different settings.
  • I can explain why state and national rules sometimes conflict.
  • I can defend an AI-use rule with evidence from the unit.

Five beats of the story

  1. Four layers of rules. Sort example rules among individual, school, company, and government.
  2. Indiana already labels some AI election media. What is one benefit and one weakness of a disclosure rule?
  3. Fifty states and Washington. Who should decide when rules conflict?
  4. Classroom rules already exist. Try the question 'Should AI be allowed on homework?' or use this sample reply: 'It may help students learn but should not replace their own work.' Ask whose view is missing.
  5. Write the rule, then test it. Write one sentence you would propose for ZCHS, then challenge it with an edge case.

Clip and fallback

No required film. Teach through the readings and decision scenarios.

Preview playback, duration and caption availability before students arrive; printed chapter notes are the fallback. Avoid video embedding.
