Teacher briefing / goals, story, and preparation

What should we check before believing an election message?

Learning targets

  • I can explain how a campaign message directs attention.
  • I can check a claim against an independent source.
  • I can distinguish a poll, a promise, and a verified result.

Success criteria

  • I can identify a message’s claim, audience, and missing context.
  • I can name a second source and explain what it supports, contradicts, or leaves unresolved.
  • I can explain why a poll or promise does not establish what will happen.

The story to tell

Model one exact claim in a think-aloud. Ask what document or record could test it. The constructed messages show different priorities without inventing real campaign quotations. Then students check one actual message in writing. Evidence may support part of a claim and leave the rest uncertain; that is a useful finding. Apply the same questions to every party.

Watch for this misconception

Do not grade political preferences or treat repeated press-release coverage as independent corroboration.

Prepare before students arrive

  • Open the student lesson, projector window, and assigned clip windows. Check captions and the reading fallback.
  • Use the background as a fallback if a clip is blocked.
  • Have the Canvas Text Entry assignment ready; the event and assignment copy tools are at the bottom of this page.
  • Use the prepared real claim, independent record, and poll card in Chapter 4. Preview the short text packet; the full campaign report is optional.
  • Finish unit work before lunch. Keep lunch untimed. Return to CNN10, then The Desk.

Assessment notes and access options

Campaign promise; Poll estimate; Official result; Claim needing checking; Claim needing checking. Assess source specificity and the match between evidence and conclusion.

Look for accurate facts, attributed evidence, and reasoning. Accept concise writing, voice typing, or the printable paper activity under the student’s accommodations. No student report-out is required. The teacher can voice anonymous examples.

How this investigation is assessed / 5 points

Political preferences are not scored. Short writing, dictated text, and agreed paper responses can demonstrate the same learning.

  • Message and framing. Identify a message’s claim, intended audience, and missing context without presenting a constructed teaching example as a real quotation.
  • Check the real claim. Record the actual claim and source/date/link, name independent evidence, and explain exactly what it supports, contradicts, or leaves unresolved.
  • Polls, promises, uncertainty. Explain why a sample-based poll does not establish an election winner and why a campaign promise does not guarantee a policy change.
  • Explain your thinking. Make the evidence-to-conclusion connection understandable. Political preference, response length, and public speaking are not scored.
