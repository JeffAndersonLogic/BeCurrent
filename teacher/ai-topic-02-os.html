TEACHER RUN OF SHOW / AI2 / 2026-11-19

What Exactly AI Is

How can a computer sound like it understands you?

Class opening

On November 17, NOVA showed AI learning patterns from huge numbers of examples. Today you learn how to use AI well, then test it yourself in a five-round challenge against a MagicSchool chatbot, built around something you are into: can you predict what it will say, can you trap it, and can you tell when it deserves your trust?

Your opener: the overview story

  • On Tuesday NOVA showed us AI that learns patterns from enormous numbers of examples, not rules somebody typed in one by one.
  • A chatbot builds its reply a piece at a time, choosing what is likely to come next. That is why it can sound like it understands you.
  • Sounding sure and being right are not the same thing. Today you find out where that line is.
  • The bot starts with AI Basics: what AI is, how to write a strong prompt, good habits, and red lines. Our rule is help, not hand-in. Then you play a five-round game against it, on your own, built around something you are into. Pick something you know a lot about, because your knowledge is your weapon.
  • Getting in, on the projector: open today's AI2 assignment in Canvas, follow the MagicSchool link, sign in, and open Can You Outsmart the Machine? Type READY. The bot teaches you how to talk to it before the game starts, and HELP works at any time.
  • When the bot sends you back, write on the BeCurrent page. The chat is not your submission; the Canvas paste is.

Timing / 55-minute before-lunch route

This version runs long by design. 0–5 min: your opener (below), ending with a one-minute demo of getting into the room on the projector. 5–8 min: Learn the Controls and Pick Your Turf in the chat. 8–20 min: AI Basics, four hands-on stops ending in an AI Driver's License. Walk the room here; this is where first-timers get stuck and where the red lines land. 20–50 min: Rounds 1–5; students pause to answer Questions 1–3 after Round 2, 4–6 after Round 3, and 7 after Round 5. 50–55 min: Question 8 and Gather All My Work. Students who do not finish submit to Canvas before next class (the board's homework line already says so). Fast finishers type BONUS. If there is no time for the closing think-aloud, open AI3 with it: it sets up that lesson.

MagicSchool chatbot setup

Create one custom chatbot in the BeCurrent MagicSchool room and paste these four fields. The instructions never say which Round 3 question is the trap, so whether the bot catches it is a real result. Put the room link in the Canvas assignment, never on this public site.

If MagicSchool is down

If MagicSchool is down for everyone: teach AI Basics from the student page's chapter 1 (the three-part prompt recipe and the red lines) yourself. Students write Round 1 predictions on paper and use the printed sample completions on the page for Round 2. Run Round 3 yourself on the projector in MagicSchool or another district-approved chatbot, typing the three questions one at a time, and students answer Questions 4–6 from what they see. Skip Rounds 4–5 and the bonus quests.

Closing think-aloud

If AI can give you an answer that sounds completely believable, how do you know when to trust it? Read three or four answers aloud with names removed, then answer it yourself out loud. Nobody is called on. It sets up AI3.

Wrap-up questions (projector: last slide)

Students pick one and think it through or jot it in their notes. Read a few written answers aloud with names removed, then think aloud yourself. Questions 8 and 9 lead into AI3.

  1. Quick check: How often did you guess the machine's next word? What does that say about its answers?
  2. Quick check: Finish it: "The chatbot is good at ___ but not at ___."
  3. Quick check: Which red line is easiest to break by accident?
  4. Go deeper: Which answer sounded sure but shouldn't have? What would a careful answer sound like?
  5. Go deeper: Why doesn't asking the chatbot again count as checking it?
  6. Go deeper: Was the bot easier to catch on a topic you know well? What does that mean for topics you don't?
  7. Go deeper: When is a good guess good enough, and when isn't it?
  8. Next time: AI3: A chatbot is wrong and someone uses the answer anyway. Who is responsible?
  9. Next time: AI3: What changes when someone uses AI to fool people on purpose?

Learning objective

  • I can explain what a chatbot is, how it predicts, and how to prompt it well.
  • I can tell a checkable fact from an opinion and from a question built on a false assumption.
  • I can explain why a convincing sentence might still be incorrect.

Five beats of the story

  1. Before you open MagicSchool: How a chatbot answers. Listen to the opener. Then open the BeCurrent MagicSchool room from today's Canvas assignment, choose Can You Outsmart the Machine?, and type READY. Earn your AI Driver's License, then play. Keep this page open beside the chat.
  2. Rounds 1 and 2: Think like the machine, then try to stump it. Play Rounds 1 and 2, including Stump the Machine. When the chatbot tells you to pause, answer Questions 1–3 here, then type NEXT in the chat.
  3. Round 3: The confidence trap, then build your own. Play Round 3 and build your own trap. Check at least one answer with a source outside the chatbot. When the chatbot tells you to pause, answer Questions 4–6 here, then type NEXT.
  4. Rounds 4 and 5: AI in your world, then you be the judge. Play Rounds 4 and 5 in the chat, then answer Question 7 here, using your best prompt from AI Basics.
  5. Checkpoint: When should we trust the machine?. Answer Question 8, review all eight answers, then Gather All My Work and submit to Canvas before lunch. Then try a bonus quest.
