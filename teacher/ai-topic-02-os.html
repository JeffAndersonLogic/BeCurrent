What Exactly AI Is

How can a computer sound like it understands you?

Class opening

On November 17, NOVA showed AI learning patterns from huge numbers of examples. Today you test that yourself in a five-round challenge against a MagicSchool chatbot, built around something you are into: can you predict what it will say, can you trap it, and can you tell when it deserves your trust?

Your opener: the overview story

  • On Tuesday NOVA showed us AI that learns patterns from enormous numbers of examples, not rules somebody typed in one by one.
  • A chatbot builds its reply a piece at a time, choosing what is likely to come next. That is why it can sound like it understands you.
  • Sounding sure and being right are not the same thing. Today you find out where that line is.
  • You are playing a five-round game against a MagicSchool chatbot, on your own. It starts by asking what you are into. Pick something you know a lot about, because your knowledge is your weapon: you will try to stump it and trap it on your own turf.
  • When the bot sends you back, write on the BeCurrent page. The chat is not your submission; the Canvas paste is.

Timing / 55-minute before-lunch route

0–5 min: your opener (below). 5–42 min: Can You Outsmart the Machine? in MagicSchool, individually, built around each student's chosen interest. Students pause in the chat to answer Questions 1–3 after Round 2 and 4–6 after Round 3, and Question 7 after Round 5. 42–50 min: Question 8, Gather All My Work and Canvas filing; fast finishers type BONUS for an ungraded bonus quest. 50–55 min: read three or four answers aloud with names removed, then think aloud on the closing question.

MagicSchool chatbot setup

Create one custom chatbot in the BeCurrent MagicSchool room and paste these four fields. The instructions never say which Round 3 question is the trap, so whether the bot catches it is a real result. Put the room link in the Canvas assignment, never on this public site.

If MagicSchool is down

If MagicSchool is down for everyone: students write Round 1 predictions on paper and use the printed sample completions on the page for Round 2. Run Round 3 yourself on the projector in MagicSchool or another district-approved chatbot, typing the three questions one at a time, and students answer Questions 4–6 from what they see. Rounds 4 and 5 become Question 7 on the page; skip the bonus quests.

Closing think-aloud

If AI can give you an answer that sounds completely believable, how do you know when to trust it? Read three or four answers aloud with names removed, then answer it yourself out loud. Nobody is called on. It sets up AI3.

Learning objective

  • I can explain patterns, training examples, and prediction in plain language.
  • I can tell a checkable fact from an opinion and from a question built on a false assumption.
  • I can explain why a convincing sentence might still be incorrect.

Five beats of the story

  1. Before you open MagicSchool: How a chatbot answers. Listen to the opener. Then open the BeCurrent MagicSchool room from today's Canvas assignment, choose Can You Outsmart the Machine?, and type READY. Pick an interest you actually know a lot about. Keep this page open beside the chat.
  2. Rounds 1 and 2: Think like the machine, then try to stump it. Play Rounds 1 and 2, including Stump the Machine. When the chatbot tells you to pause, answer Questions 1–3 here, then type NEXT in the chat.
  3. Round 3: The confidence trap, then build your own. Play Round 3 and build your own trap. Check at least one answer with a source outside the chatbot. When the chatbot tells you to pause, answer Questions 4–6 here, then type NEXT.
  4. Rounds 4 and 5: AI in your world, then you be the judge. Play Rounds 4 and 5 in the chat, then answer Question 7 here.
  5. Checkpoint: When should we trust the machine?. Answer Question 8, review all eight answers, then Gather All My Work and submit to Canvas before lunch. Then try a bonus quest.
