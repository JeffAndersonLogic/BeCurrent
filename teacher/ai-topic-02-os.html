TEACHER RUN OF SHOW / AI2 / 2026-11-19

When AI Gets it Wrong

Who should check a convincing answer?

Class opening

If an AI invents a court case, who is responsible when somebody files it with a judge?

Timing / 55-minute before-lunch route

0–4 min opening; 4–15 min read and orient; 15–29 min evidence investigation; 29–40 min practice and answers; 40–50 min checkpoint/review; 50–55 min Canvas filing.

Learning objective

  • I can identify the source of a claim.
  • I can test an AI-generated claim against independent evidence.
  • I can distinguish model error, bias, and deliberate deception.

Five beats of the story

  1. The confident answer problem. Review the same learning question from AI1: What made an answer sound trustworthy?
  2. The lawyers who failed to verify. Look at the case source: what were the attorneys responsible for checking?
  3. Bias is a different failure. Where might unfairness enter: examples, design, or human use?
  4. The fake voice that reached voters. Why could a fake voice undermine confidence even after it is uncovered?
  5. Your three-step verification habit. Complete an evidence check using an original source linked below.

Clip and fallback

A.I. Revolution | The Danger of AI Fakes ↗; assigned: 37:20 to approximately 41:30 (teacher preview required). How could an altered image or voice be made believable, and how could you check it?

Preview playback, duration and caption availability before students arrive; printed chapter notes are the fallback. Avoid video embedding.
