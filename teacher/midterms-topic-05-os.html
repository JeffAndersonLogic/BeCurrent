Election Night: What Happens Next?

55 minutes before lunch · 20–25 minutes of news after lunch · Civics

Student view ↗Open projector ↗
Teacher briefing / goals, story, and preparation

How did the 2026 midterm elections change who has power, and what could that mean for the country?

Learning targets

  • I can distinguish a projection, unofficial count, and certified result.
  • I can connect the 2026 outcome to a governing consequence and a limit.
  • I can support an explanation with two sources and a fair alternative interpretation.

Success criteria

  • I can record House, Senate, and one Indiana result or unresolved race with sources, reporting stage, and a check date.
  • I can explain two possible governing effects and one outcome that is not guaranteed.
  • I can use two identified sources, address a competing interpretation, and state an uncertainty.

The story to tell

This is the payoff. Open the results and make a dated record of what is known. A race can remain unresolved; that is part of the lesson. The 2020 video explains race calling, not 2026 winners. Connect verified outcomes to chamber leadership, committees, oversight, and lawmaking. Distinguish what happened from what might happen. The final explanation includes a fair competing interpretation and uncertainty.

Watch for this misconception

No prefilled winners. Presidential Electoral College maps do not establish congressional control; news calls are not certification.

Prepare before students arrive

  • Open the student lesson, projector window, and both core clips. Check captions and school playback.
  • Use the background as a fallback if a clip is blocked.
  • Have the Canvas Text Entry assignment ready; paste-ready bodies are linked below.
  • Teach after November 3. Open current House, Senate, and Indiana result sources. Note their update times and reporting stages. Results are not loaded automatically.
  • Finish unit work before lunch. Keep lunch untimed. Return to CNN10, then The Desk.

Assessment notes and access options

Poll / forecast; Unofficial count; News race call; Certified result; Unofficial count. Final answers depend on sourced 2026 evidence, not a fixed party answer key.

Look for accurate facts, attributed evidence, and reasoning. Accept concise writing, voice typing, or the printable paper activity under the student’s accommodations. No student report-out is required. The teacher can voice anonymous examples.

Final explanation rubric, 30 points: accurate sourced results (8); two possible institutional effects and a limit (8); two-source reasoning (6); fair competing interpretation (4); stated uncertainty (4). Political preference is not scored.
