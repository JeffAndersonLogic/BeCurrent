Election Results: What Happened and Why?

55 minutes before lunch · 20–25 minutes of news after lunch · Civics

Student view ↗Open projector ↗
Teacher briefing / goals, story, and preparation

Why did the 2026 midterms turn out the way they did, and what did voters decide here at home?

Learning targets

  • I can use facts from a news article to explain why an election turned out the way it did.
  • I can explain how voters’ worries about prices, corruption, and checks on power affected their votes.
  • I can find local and state election results in official sources and say where each one came from.

Success criteria

  • I can answer questions about the article using its numbers and examples in my own words.
  • I can explain why something happened in the election, not just what happened.
  • I can name the winner of a Boone County or Indiana race and the source where I found it.

The story to tell

Tuesday the class watched the votes come in; today they find out what happened and why. The clips give the headline, the article gives the reasons from a survey of real voters, and Part 3 brings it home to the races on Zionsville’s own ballot. The through line is sourcing: a TV host’s analysis, a news report, a voter poll and an official county results file are four different kinds of evidence.

Watch for this misconception

Do not ask students how they or their families voted or feel about the result. The MS NOW clip is a host’s analysis with a point of view: name that out loud as a sourcing lesson, not as a side. Score accuracy and reasoning, never a position. Results this week are unofficial until certified.

Prepare before students arrive

  • Open the student lesson, projector window, and assigned clip windows. Check captions and the reading fallback.
  • Use the background as a fallback if a clip is blocked.
  • Have the Canvas Text Entry assignment ready; the event and assignment copy tools are at the bottom of this page.
  • CNN 10 opens the day, then the two analysis clips back to back. Release students to read the article on their own screens (read-aloud and voice typing are fine), answer Parts 1 and 2, then use the results links for Part 3. Protect the last ten minutes to finish, then Gather All My Work and the Canvas paste.
  • The two YouTube clips’ run times were not checked automatically. Preview them before class and trim if needed. CNN 10 opens the day today, so after lunch go straight to The Desk.
  • Finish unit work before lunch. Keep lunch untimed. Return to CNN10, then The Desk.

Assessment notes and access options

Parts 1 and 2 have no single answer key: look for the article’s facts in the student’s own words plus a because. Part 3 answers come from the Boone County results file and the state results site; check them against the county file before grading.

Look for accurate facts, attributed evidence, and reasoning. Accept concise writing, voice typing, or the printable paper activity under the student’s accommodations. No student report-out is required. The teacher can voice anonymous examples.

How this investigation is assessed / 5 points

Political preferences are not scored. Short writing, dictated text, and agreed paper responses can demonstrate the same learning.

  • Uses the article. Parts 1 and 2 use the article’s numbers and examples, in your own words.
  • Explains why. Answers give a reason (because, so, which led to), not only a fact.
  • Finds the results. Part 3 names the results and the source for each one, and keeps unofficial results labeled as unofficial.
