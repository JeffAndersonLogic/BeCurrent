The unit question

Was the 2026 Iran War mainly the result of recent decisions, or decades of unresolved conflict?

Your grade depends on your reasons, not on the side you choose. You may answer “both,” but you must say which one explains more.

The rule

A date is not a cause by itself. To call an event a cause, explain how it led to what came next.

Read the question and the rule aloud. Key term: a causal mechanism is the process that connects one development to the next.

Quick think-aloud: if a car crash happens at 3:14 p.m., naming the time does not explain the crash. A start date and a cause answer different questions.

Refresher · 1953

Foreign involvement shaped how Iranians view the West.

What happened

Iran took control of its oil industry from a British company. Secret U.S. and British support helped remove Prime Minister Mohammad Mosaddegh and gave the Shah more power.

Why it matters later

For many Iranians, 1953 became proof that foreign powers would interfere when Iran acted against their interests.

1953 helps explain distrust. It did not make later war certain.

Optional refresher. Emphasize proportional causation: a historical grievance can shape later expectations without controlling every later decision.

Question to think aloud: what is the mechanism between a 1953 intervention and distrust decades later?

Source: U.S. Office of the Historian, FRUS Iran 1951 to 1954, history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1951-54IranEd2/d363. Image: Wikimedia Commons, Category: Mohammad Mosaddegh in 1951.

Refresher · 1979

The alliance ended, and both sides became distrustful.

The revolution removed the Shah and created the Islamic Republic. Soon afterward, Iranian students seized the U.S. embassy and held American hostages.

Iranian memory

Iranians remember 1953 as foreign interference and U.S. support for an unpopular ruler.

American memory

Americans remember the hostage crisis as an attack on Americans and the end of an alliance.

Both memories affected every later negotiation.

Optional refresher. Avoid presenting either memory as the complete story. The goal is perspective, and the way competing grievances reinforce one another.

Question to think aloud: which memory would make later compromise harder, and why?

Source: U.S. Office of the Historian, Nov. 4, 1979 hostage-crisis record, history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v11p1/d1.

Refresher · 1980–88 and 2015–18

A new security strategy, then a deal that failed

1980–88

Invasion and new tools

Iran survived a long invasion and felt surrounded. The Revolutionary Guard grew. Missiles and armed partner groups became more important.

2015

The nuclear deal (JCPOA)

Iran accepted nuclear limits and inspections. In return, other countries lifted many sanctions. Compromise was possible.

2018

U.S. withdrawal

The United States left the agreement and brought back sanctions. Iran later went past several nuclear limits.

The 2015 deal shows that the later conflict could have been avoided.

Optional refresher. Deterrence means discouraging attack by making the cost look too high. The same strategy can feel defensive from inside Iran and threatening from outside it.

Stress scope: the JCPOA covered nuclear limits and inspections. It did not settle the wider rivalry, missiles, or regional armed partners.

Source: IAEA verification report, iaea.org/sites/default/files/documents/gov2017-10.pdf.

2024

Direct exchanges

Threshold: the line between actions leaders will take and actions they consider too dangerous.

Optional refresher. The size of an attack matters, but so does the precedent: each round changes what leaders expect in the next.

Question to think aloud: which mattered more, the size of an escalation or the precedent it created?

Step 3 · Decide

Two ways to answer “When did the war begin?”

When the fighting started

February 28, 2026

The fighting started on this date. This answers the question “When did the fighting start?”

What explains it

An earlier turning point

A turning point answers a different question: “What earlier event made this war more likely?”

A date is not a cause by itself. Explain how the event led to what came next.

Students should hold both ideas at once: a war has a literal start date and an explanatory history.

Counterarguments

Use one of these as the other side of your argument.

Long-term · old distrust

Trust broke down after 1953 and 1979. Without that distrust, later crises might have been easier to solve.

2015 to 2018 · the broken deal

The 2015 deal showed that war could be avoided. So the end of the deal in 2018 may matter more than older events.

2024 to 2026 · recent attacks

Leaders from 2024 to 2026 made choices, such as direct attacks, that earlier leaders avoided.

A strong argument explains the best counterargument and then answers it.

These are the three rival explanations from the Topic 8 page. Model one aloud if it helps: “The 2018 withdrawal increased the risk of war because it removed verified limits, restored sanctions, and returned the dispute to pressure.” Do not model a full student answer.
