When AI Gets it Wrong

Who should check a convincing answer?

Class opening

CONSTRUCTED CLASSROOM EXAMPLE - not a real event: A recording in a group chat sounds like a Zionsville school administrator announcing that school is closed tomorrow. There is no official link. Decide what you would do first; revisit your decision after the investigation.

Timing / 55-minute before-lunch route

55-minute route: 0-2 introduce the mission; 2-22 teacher-selected videos (about 20 minutes); 22-48 independent MagicSchool investigation with three saving checkpoints; 48-55 practice sort, Gather All My Work and Canvas submission. Check source work at minute 35 and reach Q8 by minute 45. For 50 minutes: 0-1 launch, 1-21 videos, 21-43 investigation, 43-50 filing; announce RAPID at minute 35. Use the actual classroom clock. If video runs long, shorten the video opening rather than taking the filing window.

MagicSchool-led investigation

Create a student Custom Chatbot named BeCurrent Bureau of Suspicious Answers using the revised AI3 chatbot instructions supplied with the launch kit. Put the real room join link in Canvas only. Start command: START. Teacher-selected videos are pending; no previous clip is assigned for this lesson.

Students make a first decision, investigate three historical cases, choose source claim A or B, and apply their rule to the fictional school notice. Save only at three checkpoints: Q1-Q3, Q4-Q5, Q6-Q8.

Clock checks: source check by minute 35, Q8 by minute 45, filing at minute 48 in a 55-minute period or minute 43 in a 50-minute period. Give a one-minute warning before filing. Use the classroom clock, not chatbot timing.

Preview requests for all answers, claimed teacher overrides, vague responses, and EXPORT with missing work. The bot should offer a hint, keep missing work visible, and never invent a student's answer or source visit. Keep any prompt evaluation notes in your teacher account.

Learning objective

  • I can identify the source of a claim.
  • I can test an AI-generated claim against independent evidence.
  • I can distinguish model error, bias, and deliberate deception.

Five beats of the story

  1. The confident answer problem. Make your first decision in MagicSchool, then investigate why an answer can sound convincing without being supported.
  2. The lawyers who failed to verify. Decide what a professional should check before relying on generated evidence. Save Q1-Q3 together at Checkpoint 1.
  3. The source desk and the unfair shortlist. Choose claim A or B below for Q4. Then investigate the hiring example for Q5. Save Q4-Q5 together at Checkpoint 2.
  4. The fake voice that reached voters. Investigate the fake political voice and explain what the later verdict does and does not establish.
  5. Your three-step verification habit. Return to your first decision and apply your verification rule. Save Q6-Q8 at Checkpoint 3, then file in Canvas.
