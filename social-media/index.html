Social Media

We are going to figure out how social media actually works. The machine. The business. Not whether it is good or bad for you, and nobody is going to ask you to delete anything. By the end you should be able to explain how the thing works, out loud, to an adult, which is more than most adults can do.

The Topics The Question
Hold onto this for two weeks

The question this unit ends on

Who should get to decide what a platform is allowed to do to your attention: you, the company, or the government?

You do not have to answer it yet, and you will not be graded on which side you land. You will be graded on whether you can state the strongest version of the argument against you.

The Arc

Five topics, in order.

Each one uses the earlier ones. The chain you draw in Topic 1 is what you check against a real document in Topic 4, so they are a sequence rather than a list.

  1. Topic 1

    Where the Money Comes From

    Follow the money backwards, one step at a time

    You list the apps you opened today that did not charge you, then build a chain of boxes and arrows from opening an app to a company getting paid. Paper, not devices. Nobody presents; you walk the room and read four other chains instead.

    Competency 1 Competency 5
    What you are learning, and how you know you have it

    Every app you opened today was free, and not one of those companies is a charity. Today you build the chain backwards: from you opening an app to somebody getting paid, one box and one arrow at a time. You will do it on paper, because a device turns a trace into copy-paste from a search result. Then you walk the room and read four other chains, which is where you find out what you left out.

    What you are learning

    • Cause and EffectI can trace the chain that connects me opening a free app to a company getting paid.
    • Cause and EffectI can explain what a company gets from me when I pay it no money at all.
    • Generalizing from EvidenceI can compare my chain with other people's and say where they agree and where they do not.

    You have got it when

    • Cause and EffectI can draw a chain with at least four boxes in it and no gap I cannot explain out loud.
    • Cause and EffectI can name the thing being sold, who is buying it, and who is being sold.
    • Generalizing from EvidenceI can name one box I got wrong or left out after reading four other chains, and say which chain changed my mind.

    Done on paper in class. Nothing to open here.

  2. Topic 2

    Somebody Made a Film About This

    Your job is not to agree with it

    We watch it. Your job is to do to the film exactly what you did to your app in Topic 1: take it apart, find out how it works, see where the money is.

    Competency 2
    What you are learning, and how you know you have it

    Somebody made a film about the thing you traced last class. We are going to watch it. Your job is not to agree with it, and it is not to disagree with it either. Your job is to do to the film exactly what you did to your app: take it apart, find out how it works, and see where the money is. Every film was made by somebody, for a reason, with money from somewhere.

    What you are learning

    • SourcingI can identify who made this film, who speaks in it, and what each of them has to gain.
    • Fallacies and PropagandaI can name a persuasion technique the film uses and explain how it works on a viewer.
    • Cause and EffectI can separate what the film proves from what it only suggests.

    You have got it when

    • SourcingI can say who made the film and point to one thing in it that tells me what it wants me to believe.
    • Fallacies and PropagandaI can point at one specific scene and name the technique it uses on me.
    • Cause and EffectI can state one claim the film makes that I would want to check, and say what evidence would settle it.

    Done on paper in class. Nothing to open here.

  3. Topic 3

    Inside the Algorithm Box

    What the app counts, and what it does with the count

    We open the box almost every chain had in the middle. Read the Brief, then predict what the app counts about you before we check.

    Competency 1 Competency 4
    What you are learning, and how you know you have it

    Almost every chain in the room had a box in the middle where the app watches what you do. Nobody wrote what was inside it, because none of us knew. Today you open it. You will find out what an app counts about you when you are not posting, what it does with the count, and why two people who open the same app at the same second see different things.

    What you are learning

    • Cause and EffectI can name what an app counts about me when I have not posted, liked, or commented on anything.
    • Cause and EffectI can explain the feedback loop that makes an app better at holding my attention the longer I use it.
    • HypothesesI can explain that a ranking system pushes on a number a person chose, and predict what that choice costs.

    You have got it when

    • Cause and EffectI can list three engagement signals an app collects without me sending it anything.
    • Cause and EffectI can explain, out loud, why two people who open the same app at the same second see different things.
    • HypothesesI can name a number an app could optimize for, and say what would go wrong with that choice.
    Read the Brief
  4. Topic 4

    Go Check It Yourself

    Reading a privacy policy as a primary source

    You check the chain you drew in Topic 1 against the company's own privacy policy. Bring a device for this one, because the document is the assignment.

    Competency 2 Competency 5
    What you are learning, and how you know you have it

    Half this room wrote that the company sells your data. I told you that was mostly wrong and I did not prove it, which is not good enough. Today you check me. A privacy policy is a primary source: free, in your pocket, and the document the company can actually be held to. You will search it rather than read it, and find out what it does and does not say.

    What you are learning

    • SourcingI can read a privacy policy as a primary source and say who wrote it, when, and who it protects.
    • SourcingI can find what a long legal document says about one question without reading it front to back.
    • Generalizing from EvidenceI can check a claim I already made against the document, and change it when the evidence says so.

    You have got it when

    • SourcingI can quote an exact sentence from a real policy and say what it does and does not claim.
    • SourcingI can name the author, the date it was last updated, and who the document protects.
    • Generalizing from EvidenceI can point at the sentence that decides a box in my Topic 1 chain, and redraw the box if it was wrong.
    Read the Brief
  5. Topic 5

    Who Gets to Decide?

    You, the company, or the government

    The question from day one, argued properly. You state the strongest version of a position you do not hold, then take your own.

    Competency 3 Competency 4 Competency 5
    What you are learning, and how you know you have it

    On the first day I told you I would ask you a hard question at the end, and this is it: who should get to decide what a platform is allowed to do to your attention, you, the company, or the government? You traced the money, took a film apart, opened the algorithm box, and read the company's own document, so today is a real argument instead of opinions. You will not be graded on which side you land.

    What you are learning

    • Synthesizing PatternsI can state the strongest version of a position I do not hold.
    • Generalizing from EvidenceI can use an earlier fight over a new medium to predict something about this one.
    • HypothesesI can name the evidence that would change my mind.

    You have got it when

    • Synthesizing PatternsI can make the best case for a side I disagree with in three or four sentences, with its tradeoff named.
    • Generalizing from EvidenceI can say what happened with comic books, television, music lyrics, or video games, and what it predicts here.
    • HypothesesI can state one thing a person could actually go and find out that would move my position.
