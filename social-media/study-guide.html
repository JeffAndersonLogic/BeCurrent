The Social Media Assessment

Know the ideas. Build the connections.

This guide tells you what matters without giving you a test key. Your job is to organize the evidence and ideas into something you can actually use.

Essential Question

Who should get to decide what a platform is allowed to do to your attention: you, the company, or the government?

What this is

A map of the concepts, research, examples, and connections that matter for the assessment.

What this is not

An answer key. The assessment asks you to reason with these ideas, not simply recognize copied sentences.

01
The Business Model

The Attention Economy

Concepts to Review

  • Free social-media platforms
  • Advertising
  • Users vs. customers
  • Attention as something valuable
  • Revenue
  • The relationship between a business model and platform design

Questions to Think Through

  • If an app is free, how does the company still make money?
  • Who is actually paying the social-media company?
  • What are advertisers paying to gain access to?
  • Why is your attention valuable to a platform?
  • How can the way a company makes money influence the way its platform is designed?
Attention → Advertising → Revenue
02
Inside the Feed

Algorithms, Ranking & Personalization

Concepts to Review

  • Behavioral signals
  • Ranked feeds
  • Chronological feeds
  • Recommendation systems
  • Personalization
  • Algorithms
  • Optimization
  • Engagement

Questions to Think Through

  • What can a platform learn from your behavior even if you never post, like, or comment?
  • Why might how long you stop on something matter?
  • How does a ranked feed differ from a chronological feed?
  • Why can two people open the same app and see completely different content?
  • Who determines what the system is supposed to optimize?
Behavior → Signals → Predictions → Ranking → More Behavior
03
Media Literacy

Claims, Sources & Evidence

Concepts to Review

  • Source credibility
  • Incentives
  • Documentary evidence
  • Dramatization
  • Illustration vs. proof
  • Evidence quality

Questions to Think Through

  • Who created a source?
  • What perspective or incentive might the creator have?
  • What evidence supports the argument?
  • Does a dramatized scene illustrate something or actually prove that it happened?
  • Why can something be persuasive without being strong evidence?
Who is making the claim? → What evidence is being offered? → What does that evidence actually establish?
04
Reading Research

Correlation, Causation & Research

Concepts to Review

  • Correlation
  • Causation
  • Alternative explanations
  • Effect size
  • Research design
  • Research interpretation

Questions to Think Through

  • What is the difference between two things occurring together and one causing the other?
  • Why can't a relationship between social-media use and anxiety automatically establish causation?
  • What other explanations might fit the same evidence?
  • Why does the size of an observed relationship matter?
  • Why might honest researchers reach different conclusions?
“These things are related.” ≠ “This thing caused that thing.”
05
Named Research

Amy Orben & Andrew Przybylski — 2019

Example to Know

  • What relationship they were investigating
  • What they found about screen use and teenage wellbeing
  • Why the size of the association mattered
  • Why comparisons to eating potatoes or wearing glasses mattered

Bigger Connection

  • The existence of a statistical relationship does not automatically tell you whether it is large enough to explain the claims people are making about it.
  • Think about the difference between statistically detectable and practically significant.
06
Your Data

Digital Footprint, Privacy & Security

Concepts to Review

  • Digital footprint
  • Permanence
  • Screenshots, reposts, archives, and backups
  • Strong and unique passwords
  • Two-factor authentication
  • Platform data collection

Questions to Think Through

  • Why should something posted online be treated as potentially permanent?
  • What risks do passwords and 2FA help reduce?
  • What is the difference between protecting an account from an outsider and controlling what the platform itself collects?
Account Security ≠ Platform Privacy

08
Named Research

Vosoughi, Roy & Aral — 2018

Example to Know

  • What the researchers studied
  • How false stories compared with true stories
  • What happened when automated accounts were removed from the analysis
  • What that suggested about the role of human users
  • Why novelty and emotion mattered

Bigger Connection

  • False information does not necessarily need a machine to spread it.
  • Human choices matter.
Novelty + Emotion + Human Sharing
09
Verification

Lateral Reading & Professional Fact-Checking

Example to Know

  • Stanford researchers compared professional fact-checkers with students.
  • Professional fact-checkers often left the original page quickly to investigate the publisher elsewhere.

Questions to Think Through

  • What is lateral reading?
  • Why might a professional fact-checker leave a webpage rather than simply reading it more carefully?
  • Why aren't professional design, logos, and About pages enough to establish credibility?
“What does this website say about itself?” → “What do reliable outside sources say about this website or claim?”
10
Politics & Participation

Social Media, Elections & Microtargeting

Concepts to Review

  • Election misinformation
  • Voting procedures
  • Political persuasion
  • Participation
  • Microtargeting
  • Transparency
  • Political advertising

Questions to Think Through

  • Why might false information about voting procedures be particularly damaging?
  • How is influencing whether someone participates different from persuading someone which candidate to support?
  • How does microtargeting differ from a broad public advertisement?
  • Why does transparency matter?
Influencing a voter's choice ≠ Influencing whether a voter participates
11
The Unit Question

Tradeoffs & Who Should Make the Rules?

Three Decision-Makers

  • The User: choice, personal responsibility, controls, limits of individual willpower
  • The Company: expertise, ability to change the product, self-regulation, financial incentives
  • The Government: common rules, protection, enforcement, government power, unintended consequences

Questions to Think Through

  • What might each option do well?
  • What might each option do poorly?
  • What does each approach gain?
  • What does each approach give up?
  • Why might company self-regulation create a conflict of interest?
Benefit → Cost → Tradeoff
12
Historical Precedent

Earlier Fights Over New Media

1954 · Comic Books1970s · Television Violence1985 · Music Lyrics1993 · Video Games

Examples to Know

  • What concerns people raised about each medium
  • How government pressure influenced the industries involved
  • What role industry self-regulation played
  • What similar outcome appeared across the examples

Bigger Connection

  • Earlier media controversies can be useful precedents, but a good comparison also asks how social media is different.
  • Look for the relationship between public concern, government pressure, and industry response.
Public Concern → Government Pressure → Industry Response
13
Platform Liability

Section 230

Concept to Review

  • The basic meaning of Section 230 as it was presented in this unit
  • Why the word publisher matters
  • The difference between the person who creates a post and the platform where it appears

Questions to Think Through

  • How are platforms generally treated when users post content on them?
  • What would change if a platform were automatically treated as the publisher of everything its users posted?
  • What does Section 230 not mean about individual responsibility?
Person who creates the post ≠ Platform where the post appears
Putting the Unit Together

The assessment is really asking whether you can follow the system.

The individual concepts matter, but the strongest preparation is being able to move from the business model to the algorithm to the information environment to the argument over responsibility.

Free Platform → Revenue → Attention → Behavioral Signals → Ranking → Personalized Content → Information & Sharing → Privacy / Politics / Responsibility → Who Should Decide?
