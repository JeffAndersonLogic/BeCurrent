The War in Ukraine · Topic 3 · Reverse History stop: 2014

The War Before the War

2022 expanded a conflict that was already eight years old. Topic 3 finds the rupture: a political crisis in Kyiv, Russia's seizure of Crimea, armed conflict in Donbas and ceasefire agreements that never produced a lasting settlement.

Teacher view: Required path for the Unit half: four stops + two short filings. Do not turn this into a full lecture on every 2014 protest or battle. The goal is for students to explain why 2014 changes the meaning of 2022.
Today's question

Why were Russia and Ukraine already fighting before 2022?

Your older presentation correctly placed Crimea and Donbas at the center. This version separates what happened from the arguments each side made about it.

Step 1 · Kyiv

A dispute over Ukraine's direction became a national political crisis.

In November 2013, President Viktor Yanukovych's government suspended preparations to sign an association agreement with the European Union. Protests grew into the Euromaidan movement. After months of confrontation and deadly violence, Yanukovych left Kyiv in February 2014 and parliament transferred presidential authority pending new elections.

NOV. 2013

EU agreement paused

The government's reversal triggered large pro-European demonstrations in Kyiv.

WINTER 2013–14

Protests widen

The demonstrations became a broader challenge to Yanukovych's government, corruption and the use of force against protesters.

FEB. 2014

Yanukovych leaves Kyiv

After deadly clashes and a collapsing political deal, the president left the capital and then Ukraine.

NEW GOVERNMENT

Russia rejects the transition

Moscow described the change in power as an unconstitutional coup; Ukraine's parliament and later elections treated it as a transfer after Yanukovych abandoned office.

Step 2 · Crimea

Within days, the crisis moved from politics to territory.

Armed forces without insignia took control of key sites in Crimea. Russia later acknowledged they were Russian troops. A referendum was held under Russian military control, and Russia annexed Crimea in March 2014.

International record · United Nations

Ukraine's borders were not legally changed.

On March 27, 2014, the UN General Assembly adopted Resolution 68/262 by a vote of 100–11–58. It affirmed Ukraine's territorial integrity within its internationally recognized borders and said the Crimea referendum could not change the peninsula's status.

What this establishes: the annexation was not accepted by the UN General Assembly as a lawful change to Ukraine's borders.

Why Crimea mattered

Territory, identity and military power overlap here.

Crimea is connected to Russian and Ukrainian historical memory and contains Sevastopol, home to Russia's Black Sea Fleet. That made the peninsula strategically important as well as politically symbolic.

Reverse History thread: Crimea joins all three unit threads — sovereignty, security and territory.

Your first filing

Why is the UN resolution useful evidence?

In two or three sentences, explain what Resolution 68/262 tells you about Crimea's legal status and one thing it cannot tell you about why Russia chose to act.

Step 3 · Donbas

The conflict spread into eastern Ukraine — and did not end.

In spring 2014, armed pro-Russian groups seized government buildings in parts of Donetsk and Luhansk. Fighting grew between Ukrainian forces and Russian-backed separatist forces. Russia provided military, political and material support; Russian forces also intervened directly at key points in the conflict.

APRIL 2014

Armed seizures

Separatist groups declared self-proclaimed “people's republics” in Donetsk and Luhansk.

SUMMER 2014

War intensifies

Fighting became a sustained armed conflict rather than a temporary political standoff.

SEPT. 2014 / FEB. 2015

Minsk agreements

Negotiators tried to create ceasefires and a political process. The agreements reduced some fighting at times but never produced a durable settlement.

The key insight: 2014 creates the war that 2022 expands.

By the end of 2014, Russia controlled Crimea, separatist-held areas of Donetsk and Luhansk were outside Kyiv's control, and Ukraine and Russia were locked into a conflict that diplomacy had not resolved.
