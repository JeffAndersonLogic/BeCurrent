The War in Ukraine · Topic 2 · Reverse History stop: 2022

The Full-Scale Invasion

February 24, 2022 did not begin the Russia–Ukraine conflict. It transformed an eight-year regional war into a full-scale invasion of Ukraine. Today's question is narrower: what changed in 2022, and what did Russia say it was trying to accomplish?

What you should leave with

One date. Three distinctions.

By the end of this topic, the goal is not to know every battle of 2022. It is to make three distinctions that the rest of the unit depends on.

2022 was an escalation, not the beginning.

Russia and Ukraine had already been in conflict since 2014. The full-scale invasion expanded the geography, military scale and international consequences of that war.

A stated reason is evidence of a claim — not proof the claim is true.

Putin's speeches tell us what the Russian government said it was doing. Other records are needed to test those claims.

Step 1 · Warning signs

The invasion did not arrive without warning.

Your older presentation correctly started this section with the late-2021 troop buildup. Keep the sequence tight: buildup, demands, recognition, invasion.

LATE 2021

Russia masses forces around Ukraine.

Russian units, armor and artillery accumulated near Ukraine and in occupied Crimea. U.S. and allied governments publicly warned that the buildup could support a major invasion.

DECEMBER 2021

Moscow demands a different European security order.

Russia proposed security arrangements that included stopping further NATO enlargement and limiting NATO military activity in countries that joined after the Cold War.

FEBRUARY 21, 2022

Russia recognizes separatist-held regions.

Putin recognized the self-declared Donetsk and Luhansk “people's republics” and ordered Russian forces into those areas. Three days later, the military operation expanded across Ukraine.

Step 2 · Source test

What Russia said is not the same as what the evidence proves.

On February 24, Putin announced what he called a “special military operation.” The speech is valuable because it records the Russian government's public case for war. It cannot verify its own claims.

Primary source · Russian presidency

Russia's public case

Putin's February 24 address connected the military action to several arguments:

  • NATO expansion and Russian security.
  • Protection of people in the Donbas.
  • Claims that Ukraine should be “demilitarized” and “denazified.”
What this proves: these were arguments the Russian president publicly used to justify the invasion. What it does not prove: that those claims were accurate or that they were the only motives behind the decision.

Read the full February 24 address →

International record · United Nations

How most UN members classified the action

On March 2, 2022, the UN General Assembly adopted Resolution ES-11/1 by a vote of 141–5–35. It reaffirmed Ukraine's sovereignty and territorial integrity, deplored Russia's aggression and demanded Russian forces withdraw.

Why this matters: it gives students a separate international record for evaluating how the invasion was understood under the UN Charter. It does not tell us every motive of every government that voted.

Step 3 · What happened

The opening attack was much larger than Donbas.

Russia attacked Ukraine from several directions. That scale is one reason February 2022 is a turning point even though the conflict itself began earlier.

N

Toward Kyiv

Russian forces entered from Belarus and pushed toward the capital. Ukraine's government remained in Kyiv, Ukrainian resistance stiffened, and Russia failed to seize the city quickly.

E

From the east

Russian forces expanded operations from Russia and the areas of Donetsk and Luhansk already outside Kyiv's control. Fighting intensified across the east.

S

From Crimea

Russian forces moved north from Crimea into southern Ukraine, linking the 2014 territorial seizure directly to the military geography of the 2022 invasion.

The quick-war expectation failed.

By early April, Russian forces had withdrawn from northern Ukraine after failing to capture Kyiv. The war then concentrated more heavily in eastern and southern Ukraine. Later Ukrainian counteroffensives recovered significant territory, but the conflict did not end.

Step 4 · What changed

2022 explains the scale of today's war. It still does not explain why the conflict existed.

This is the Reverse History handoff. Topic 2 tells us how the war expanded. Topic 3 has to explain why Crimea and Donbas were already contested eight years earlier.

The war before the war.

If February 2022 expanded an existing conflict, we now have to find the earlier rupture: Euromaidan, the fall of Viktor Yanukovych, Russia's seizure and annexation of Crimea, the uprising and war in Donbas, and the failed effort to contain the fighting through the Minsk agreements.
