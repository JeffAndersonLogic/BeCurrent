The War in Ukraine · Topic 5 · Reverse History stop: 1991–94

Independence Without Security

The Soviet Union disappeared. Ukraine became independent. But independence raised immediate questions about borders, military power, nuclear weapons and what outside countries would actually do if those borders were later threatened.

Teacher view: Required path: collapse → referendum → nuclear inheritance → Budapest source test. The key conceptual distinction is security assurance versus collective-defense guarantee.
Today's question

What problems were left unresolved when the Soviet Union disappeared?

The old presentation was right that the Soviet collapse weakened Russia's old defense system. The new lesson adds the equally important Ukrainian side: independence was overwhelmingly chosen, and the new state inherited enormous security problems.

Step 1 · 1991

An empire disappeared faster than a new security order could form.

In August 1991, after a failed hard-line coup in Moscow, Ukraine's parliament declared independence. In December, Ukraine, Russia and Belarus signed the Belavezha Agreement and declared that the Soviet Union had ceased to exist.

BORDERS

Republic lines became international borders.

Administrative boundaries inside the USSR suddenly separated independent states.

MILITARY

Soviet forces and bases were divided.

Russia lost the strategic depth of a single Soviet state while Ukraine inherited military facilities, personnel and equipment on its territory.

IDENTITY

Citizens had to define new political loyalties.

People who had lived in one Soviet system now lived in separate countries with different governments and foreign-policy choices.

Step 2 · Dec. 1, 1991

Ukraine did not barely choose independence.

In the referendum, 90.3% of participating voters supported the Act of Independence. Support was broad across the country, including more than 83% in both Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts.

Evidence · national referendum

90.3% voted yes.

The result matters because it complicates the idea that independent Ukraine was simply a western or nationalist project imposed on a Russian-leaning east.

What this proves: overwhelming public support for an independent Ukrainian state at that moment. What it does not prove: that every later political disagreement disappeared.

Donbas check

Donetsk and Luhansk also voted for independence.

In 1991, 83.90% in Donetsk oblast and 83.86% in Luhansk oblast voted yes. That does not erase later separatism; it shows that later events should not be projected backward as if they were inevitable.

Historical thinking move: later conflict does not automatically tell you what people believed decades earlier.

Step 3 · Nuclear inheritance

One of the world's largest nuclear arsenals was located in newly independent Ukraine.

When the USSR collapsed, strategic and tactical nuclear weapons were stationed on Ukrainian territory. Operational control and command arrangements were complicated and tied to the former Soviet system, but the weapons created an enormous diplomatic and security problem: what would happen to them?

Ukraine's problem

Keep, control or give up the weapons?

Keeping them would have required enormous technical, political and financial decisions and could have isolated Ukraine internationally. Giving them up raised the question of what security Ukraine would receive in return.

International problem

Prevent a new nuclear-armed state.

The United States, Russia and others pushed for Ukraine to join the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty as a non-nuclear-weapon state and for the warheads to be transferred out.

Step 4 · Dec. 5, 1994

The Budapest Memorandum promised respect and consultation — not automatic military defense.

Ukraine, Russia, the United States and the United Kingdom signed the Memorandum on Security Assurances when Ukraine joined the NPT as a non-nuclear-weapon state.

Primary source · Budapest Memorandum

What the signatories said they would do

  • Respect Ukraine's independence, sovereignty and existing borders.
  • Refrain from the threat or use of force against Ukraine.
  • Refrain from economic coercion.
  • Seek UN Security Council action if Ukraine became a victim of nuclear aggression.
  • Consult if questions arose about the commitments.

The distinction

Security assurance ≠ Article 5

The memorandum did not create a NATO-style promise that U.S. or British forces would automatically enter a war to defend Ukraine. That difference matters when students later hear the phrase “security guarantees” in peace negotiations.

Reverse History payoff: today's debate over what kind of guarantee could make Ukraine feel secure has roots in the security system created after 1991.
