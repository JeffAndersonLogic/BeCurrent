Read the background

On November 3, 2026, voters choose members of Congress without electing a president. Congress has two chambers: the House and the Senate. All 435 voting House seats face election every two years. Senators serve six-year terms, so roughly one-third of Senate seats have regular elections every two years. Special elections can add other Senate races. State and local offices can also appear on ballots.

Read the background

House members represent districts. A state’s population determines its number of House seats. Every state has two senators. Voters elect people to particular offices; there is no single nationwide congressional vote that directly awards seats to parties. The winners of separate races determine membership. A new majority can influence leaders and priorities without replacing the president.

Read the background

A primary usually helps decide a party’s nominee. The general election chooses who holds the office. A primary victory or a favorable poll does not establish a November winner. The FEC can establish the general-election date, but it cannot report winners before the election. Use official references for durable facts and identify uncertainty honestly.
