2 / On your own: campaign messages

READ / What is framing?

Campaigns choose a few images, facts, and stories to persuade voters. Those choices are called framing. An advertisement may contain accurate information but leave out important context.

READ / How do you verify a claim?

A campaign message tells you what a campaign says. To test a factual claim, find a relevant independent record such as a vote, a budget document, or a careful fact-check.

4 / Independent evidence

READ / Check the same law

Compare what the federal government funded with what it projected for Medicaid spending. The programs and time periods differ, and a financial risk is not a guaranteed closure.

READ / THE EVIDENCE / AFTER QUESTION 6

What does the full record establish?

Record 1 · New rural-health money. The federal government created the Rural Health Transformation Program with $50 billion to be distributed over five fiscal years (2026–2030). These funds support approved rural-health projects; they do not simply replace every dollar of hospital operating revenue.

Medicaid.gov / Rural Health Transformation Program ↗

Record 2 · Medicaid spending changes. The Congressional Budget Office projected roughly $900 billion in federal Medicaid spending reductions over ten years under the 2025 law. That is a national projection, with a different purpose and time span from the five-year fund, not a count of rural hospitals that will close.

Congressional Budget Office / original estimates ↗

Record 3 · Hospital closure claims. FactCheck.org found the Iowa ad cited an earlier list of financially at-risk hospitals. The list was not a prediction of how many would close because of the new law.

FactCheck.org / September 17, 2026 investigation ↗

Evidence limit: These sources establish new investment and projected spending reductions. Neither ad alone tells the full story, and the evidence does not establish a specific number of future closures. These ads address different elections and candidates but the same law.

5 / Polls and promises

READ / Not the same as a result

A poll estimates the views of a sample. A campaign promise names a future goal. Neither establishes certified votes or guarantees a new law.

Read a real poll: a snapshot, not a seat count

Method

If the election were held then, which U.S. House candidate in their district would respondents choose? Candidate names and parties were shown in most districts.

  • Democratic candidate: 44%
  • Republican candidate: 36%
  • Another candidate: 5%
  • Not sure: 15%

The registered-voter sample has a margin of sampling error of ±1.8 percentage points at 95% confidence. Wording, nonresponse, and later turnout can add uncertainty. This is not an error band for the number of seats won.
