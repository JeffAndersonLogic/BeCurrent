The Fight for Your Vote

What should we check before believing an election message?

Learning targets

  • I can explain how a campaign message directs attention.
  • I can check a claim against an independent source.
  • I can distinguish a poll, a promise, and a verified result.

Success criteria

  • I can identify a message’s claim, audience, and missing context.
  • I can name a second source and explain what it supports, contradicts, or leaves unresolved.
  • I can explain why a poll or promise does not establish what will happen.

Background

Campaigns persuade through issue choices, numbers, images, and emotional appeals. Those choices are framing. A campaign page is evidence of what a candidate says, but it is not independent proof of the claim or a guarantee the promise can be delivered. Ask who is speaking, what is claimed, what supports it, and what is missing.

Choose evidence that answers the actual claim. A vote record can establish how someone voted. A budget document can establish a reported amount. Independent reporting can add context. Two websites repeating one press release are not two independent confirmations. If evidence cannot settle a claim, label it unresolved.

A poll estimates opinion in a sampled group at a particular time. Who was asked, when, the wording, and uncertainty all matter. A national poll does not award congressional seats. A promise describes a desired future action that may require other institutions. Neither is a verified election result.

Practice

Classify these constructed teaching examples. None is an actual candidate quotation, poll, or result.

If elected, I will seek a new education law. ___________________

In this sample, 48 percent prefer Candidate A. ___________________

The election office certifies the final totals. ___________________

Our opponent always votes against families. ___________________

Our campaign says its plan will halve every household bill. ___________________

Application

Constructed scenario: A campaign says its plan will save every household money. A poll puts its candidate ahead. You have not seen the plan, the polling methods, or election results.

1. Choose a constructed message. Identify its claim, audience, and a detail you would check.

Start here: Message __ focuses on ____. Its audience is ____. I would check ____.

2. Record a real election claim and its source/date/link. Name an independent source you checked and explain what the evidence establishes or leaves unresolved.

Start here: The claim is ____ (source/date/link). I checked ____. The evidence ____.

3. Why do a favorable poll and an appealing promise fail to prove an election victory or a policy change?

Start here: A poll cannot prove ____ because ____. A promise still depends on ____.
