Election Day: What Can We Know Today?

Watch two short clips and live Election Day coverage, learn how a vote becomes a result, and sort what the coverage can and cannot tell us while the polls are still open.

Today’s question

The polls are still open while we watch. What can the coverage tell us today, and what can nobody know yet?

Learning targets and success criteria

Learning targets

  • I can explain the steps from casting a ballot to a certified result.
  • I can sort what the coverage reports into how voting works, reports from polling places, voter surveys, predictions, and claims to check.
  • I can explain why no 2026 winner can be known while the polls are open.

Success criteria

  • I can put polls open, polls close, unofficial count, news call, and certification in the right order.
  • I can name the kind of statement for things I hear in the coverage, and spot a prediction.
  • I can explain why a race call and an early lead are not the official result.

Before you watch: one rule

While the polls are open, election offices do not report vote counts, and news organizations do not call a state’s races until its polls have closed. Anything during class that sounds like a 2026 winner is a prediction, an opinion, or a mistake.

How a vote becomes a result

Every step below happens in this order. Today we are watching the first two.

  1. Polls open6 a.m. local time in Indiana. Early in-person voting ended Monday, November 2.
  2. Voting all dayIndiana voters show a photo ID issued by Indiana or the U.S. government. A voter without one can cast a provisional ballot and has ten days to show ID to the county election board.
  3. Polls close6 p.m. local time in Indiana. Anyone already in line at 6 p.m. still gets to vote. Polls in most other states close later into the night.
  4. Unofficial counts appearAfter polls close, election offices post partial totals. Different kinds of ballots are counted at different times, so an early lead can shrink or flip.
  5. News organizations call racesA call is a news organization’s conclusion that one candidate is the winner, based on counted votes and its analysis. Some races are too close to call for days.
  6. Canvass and certificationOver the next days and weeks, officials check the totals, add eligible provisional ballots, and certify the official result.
  7. New CongressThe members elected today ordinarily take office on January 3, 2027. Election Day does not instantly change Congress.

Words you will hear

Turnout

How many eligible people voted. During the day, coverage can only report early signs, like lines or check-in numbers.

Provisional ballot

A ballot set aside until officials confirm the voter is eligible. It is counted later, if it qualifies.

Voter survey

Questions asked of people who voted. It describes what voters say. It is not a count of votes.

Unofficial count

Partial totals posted on election night. Unofficial means not yet checked and certified.

Race call

A news organization’s conclusion about a winner. It is not the official result.

Too close to call

The counted votes so far do not support calling a winner. It is a normal outcome, not a sign of a problem.

Five kinds of statement

Everything the coverage says fits one of these. Sorting them in your head while you watch is today’s job.

1How voting works

A fact about the rules or the process. Example: when polls close in a state.

2From a polling place

A reporter describes what is happening somewhere: lines, weather, a broken machine, a quiet room.

3Voter survey

What voters told a survey about themselves or the issues. Not a vote count.

4Prediction or opinion

What someone thinks will happen or should happen. Nobody can check it until the votes are counted.

5Claim to check

A statement about a problem or a rumor. Wait for election officials or independent reporting before you believe it.
