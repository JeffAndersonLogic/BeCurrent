Today’s question

What should we check before believing an election message?

Learning targets and success criteria

Learning targets

  • I can explain how a campaign message directs attention.
  • I can check a claim against an independent source.
  • I can distinguish a poll, a promise, and a verified result.

Success criteria

  • I can identify a message’s claim, audience, and missing context.
  • I can name a second source and explain what it supports, contradicts, or leaves unresolved.
  • I can explain why a poll or promise does not establish what will happen.

Read the background

Campaigns persuade through issue choices, numbers, images, and emotional appeals. Those choices are framing. A campaign page is evidence of what a candidate says, but it is not independent proof of the claim or a guarantee the promise can be delivered. Ask who is speaking, what is claimed, what supports it, and what is missing.

Read the background

A poll estimates opinion in a sampled group at a particular time. Who was asked, when, the wording, and uncertainty all matter. A national poll does not award congressional seats. A promise describes a desired future action that may require other institutions. Neither is a verified election result.
