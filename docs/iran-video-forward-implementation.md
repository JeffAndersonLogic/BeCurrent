# Iran at War — Video-Forward Accessibility Implementation

**Status:** Approved for implementation  
**Branch:** `main`  
**Reviewed:** September 4, 2026  
**Course:** BeCurrent Current Events  
**Unit dates:** Sept. 8, 10, 14, 16, 18, 22, 24, 28, 2026

## Why this change

The Iran unit has a strong Reverse History causal spine, but its current student-facing delivery relies too heavily on sustained reading for a class with a large IEP/504 population. The goal is **not** to lower historical reasoning. The goal is to reduce the reading required to access the content.

New default pattern:

**WATCH → 3–5 bullet “What Matters” scaffold → filing → WATCH → evidence card → filing**

Keep the five-filings architecture. Keep the claim-builder rigor. Reduce expository reading by roughly **30–50%** where vetted video teaches the same content.

## Source standard

Preferred sources are already approved BeCurrent sources (BBC, Reuters) plus PBS NewsHour / American Experience / FRONTLINE.

PBS clears the reliability bar for classroom use: Ad Fontes rates PBS overall as **Middle / Reliable, Analysis-Fact Reporting** (43.31 reliability) and PBS NewsHour as **Reliable, Analysis/Fact Reporting** (42.00 reliability), though its NewsHour bias score skews left. AllSides currently rates PBS NewsHour **Lean Left**, with medium confidence. Therefore: use PBS primarily for reported packages, historical documentaries, archival material, and deliberately balanced expert panels—not as the sole source for contested political interpretation.

BBC and Reuters remain within the established BeCurrent sourcing standard. When a BBC or PBS segment contains analysis rather than straight reporting, label it as analysis.

## Standard video card for the student site

Every video should appear as a BeCurrent card with:

- **WATCH 1 — THE STORY / WATCH 2 — GO DEEPER / WATCH 3 — OPTIONAL EXTEND**
- source + runtime
- mandatory / optional tag
- **Why you’re watching** (one sentence)
- **Listen for** (three terms)
- **After watching** (one question that feeds the next filing)
- a direct link rather than an iframe unless embedding is verified on student Chromebooks
- CC/caption badge only when verified

Do not turn video into passive “movie time.” Every required clip must feed a filing or evidence move.

# Implementation rules

## Mandatory / optional balance
Aim for **20–30 required video minutes per day**, not 40+. Optional documentary content should be clearly labeled.

## Captions
- PBS videos marked CC above are verified through PBS metadata.
- BBC YouTube selections should be checked on the actual classroom device for caption availability before the lesson; do not display a “CC verified” badge until confirmed.

## Chromebook / blocking risk
- Use **direct external links** first.
- Do not assume PBS/BBC/YouTube iframes will render inside GitHub Pages or Canvas.
- Before publication, test every required link on a student Chromebook / student network.
- If YouTube is blocked for students but teacher playback works, label the clip **Teacher Play** and do not require independent student access.

## Reading reductions must preserve these items
Do not cut:
- primary-source evidence moments
- quantitative evidence in Topic 7
- perspective fault lines
- causal arrows / mechanisms
- all five filings
- final Claim Builder prompts

Cut first:
- duplicate narrative exposition
- multi-paragraph background that the required video already teaches
- repeated descriptions of the same event in timeline + prose + source card

## Video card language
Each required card should include a very short prompt, e.g.:

> **Why you’re watching:** Iran and Israel had fought indirectly for decades. Watch for what changes when Iran attacks Israel directly.  
> **Listen for:** threshold · deterrence · retaliation  
> **After watching:** What became possible after this event that had been less acceptable before it?

## Source-balance note for teachers
PBS NewsHour is reliable but not rated politically dead-center across all rating services. For contested policy questions, pair PBS analysis with neutral factual records already in the unit (IAEA, EIA, U.S. Office of the Historian) and with BBC/Reuters reporting. The objective is not artificial “both sides” symmetry; it is transparent sourcing and evidence separation.

# QA gate before students see the revised pages

1. Every required video opens on a student Chromebook.
2. Every required video has usable captions or a teacher-accessible transcript.
3. No required iframe depends on a provider that blocks embedding.
4. Each required video feeds a specific filing.
5. No page exceeds ~30 required video minutes without a deliberate teacher reason.
6. Reading that remains is evidence, argument structure, or a short scaffold—not duplicated exposition.
7. Topic 8 remains a synthesis day rather than becoming another content-delivery day.
8. Run the full BeCurrent test suite after page edits; preserve the Iran 9/9 contract and all structural checks.
